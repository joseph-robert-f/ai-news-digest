PolicyReported 26 Sep+

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic push a joint frontier-AI standards body (“SAFA”)

Why it matters: Rivals are writing their own safety rules before regulators do; expect shared testing and incident-reporting norms to become procurement and compliance baselines.

Detail & sources

The tentative name is Standards Authority for Frontier AI. Reports say it could launch late 2026 or early 2027 and cover third-party pre-deployment testing, incident reporting and auditor qualification. It follows Demis Hassabis’s July call for a FINRA-style body. Critics say a body built by the labs themselves raises independence concerns. This is new to this series; the 29 Sep accord covered a different, voluntary pledge.

Why it matters: Enforcement is moving from chipmakers to freight and server vendors; expect tighter customer-verification demands across the supply chain.

Dev securityOngoing

Hallucinated packages remain a live supply-chain risk for AI coding agents

Why it matters: Agents that install dependencies unattended can pull attacker-registered packages; require lockfiles, allow-lists and human review of new dependencies.

Detail & sources

Slopsquatting means attackers register package names that models invent. Research cited 19.7% of recommended packages did not exist, with 43% repeating across prompts. A July paper, HalluSquatting, showed the same idea can push coding assistants to install botnet malware. A 1 Oct agent-news roundup also flagged the concern. No new incident is claimed here.
