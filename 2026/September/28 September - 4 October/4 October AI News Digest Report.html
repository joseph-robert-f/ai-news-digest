Hawley–Murphy bill would make agent hacking a liability for operators and developers

Why it matters: if you deploy autonomous agents, legal exposure for what they do may soon sit with you; agent permissions and safeguards become a compliance item.

Detail & sources

The bipartisan AI Agent Accountability Act would use the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act to hold operators liable when they knowingly run an agent that causes damage, and would reach developers who skip reasonable safeguards. It follows the FTC probe of OpenAI, Anthropic and METR covered on 1 October (new here: the bill itself).

Regulation30 Sep

California signs the “No Robo Bosses” Act

Why it matters: any HR workflow that lets AI drive firing or discipline now needs human corroboration and written notice in California from 1 July 2027.

Detail & sources

SB 947 bars employers from relying only on automated systems to fire or discipline workers. Employers that rely mainly on AI must have a human check the decision against other inputs, and tell the worker in writing what data was used and whom to contact. Newsom vetoed a broader version in 2025.
