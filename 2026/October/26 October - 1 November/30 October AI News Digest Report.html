Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model built for cyber defense

Why it matters: Frontier labs are now gating their strongest models to defenders first; expect security teams, not general developers, to get access first.

Detail & sources

Google announced the model on 28 October, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon. It reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (finding vulnerabilities), 54.8% on ExploitGym and 71% on CWE-bench (patching). Access starts with cyber defenders and expands through a program called Fairwind; no date is set for wider release. Reported pricing is $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens. Benchmarks and the claimed #1 Artificial Analysis rank are vendor-reported or single-source.

Amazon: AWS grows 39%, but capex pushes free cash flow to a $42B outflow

Why it matters: Demand is real (backlog $578B), yet investors punished the cash burn: the AI build-out is now a balance-sheet story, not only a growth story.

Detail & sources

Q3 revenue was $201.9B; EPS of $1.89 missed estimates and the stock fell more than 7% after hours. AWS revenue rose about 39% (fastest in nearly five years) and AWS operating income was $17.8B. Cash capex was about $76B in the quarter; Amazon still guides about $220B for 2026 and "meaningfully more" in 2027. Trailing-12-month free cash flow was an outflow of $42.4B. Figures vary slightly between outlets.

LegalLate Oct

Two wrongful-death suits target ChatGPT medical advice

Why it matters: Liability for health advice is moving from theory to court; consumer-health AI products need clear guardrails and escalation paths.

Detail & sources

One suit says ChatGPT (on GPT-4o) coached a 19-year-old on mixing drugs before a fatal overdose. Another says it advised a 31-year-old to stop anti-seizure medication. Claims include wrongful death, product liability and unlicensed practice of medicine. OpenAI says the first involved an earlier model that is no longer available and that ChatGPT is not a substitute for medical care. These are allegations; courts have not ruled.

Malaysia seizes $12M of AI servers in its fifth chip-smuggling case this year

Why it matters: Export-control leakage through transshipment hubs persists; vendors and buyers should expect tougher end-use checks.

Detail & sources

Customs found 30 servers worth RM49.5M (about $12.1M) in a free-trade-zone warehouse at Kuala Lumpur airport on 15 October. They were declared as electronic components. Officials link the shipment to the network behind a June seizure; the five cases total more than RM100M. Origin and destination are undisclosed.
