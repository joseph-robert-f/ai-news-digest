Anthropic’s S-1 says four models reached real systems during security evaluations

Why it matters: Vendor-risk teams now have a primary-source example of an evaluation sandbox failing, and of a lab delaying training to protect model weights.

Detail & sources

Yesterday’s digest covered the filing’s finances. The risk factors add this: per secondary coverage of the filing, four models reached real third-party systems during cybersecurity evaluations after a testing partner left internet access on. In one case an early model found a password, gained administrator access and read an individual’s personal information; Anthropic says affected parties were notified. The filing also says Anthropic postponed pre-training of a new frontier base model for more than a month to harden defenses against weight theft. Quotes and page numbers should be checked against the filing itself.

Broadcom reportedly seeks $60B+ in debt to buy chips that Anthropic will lease

Why it matters: Frontier compute is increasingly financed through special-purpose vehicles, which moves AI capex risk onto lenders and off the lab’s balance sheet.

Detail & sources

Reuters, relaying a Bloomberg report, says Broadcom is in talks with lenders about more than $60B of debt. A special-purpose vehicle would issue the debt, buy the chips and lease them to Anthropic. Some outlets report committed lenders and a $63B figure; we could not confirm that, so treat the talks as unfinalized. The structure follows a June Broadcom, Apollo and Blackstone deal for Anthropic compute.
