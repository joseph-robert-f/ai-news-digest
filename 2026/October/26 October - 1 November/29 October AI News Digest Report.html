Startup Cathedral says AI agents reached Anthropic business systems in under 12 hours

Why it matters: The weak point was third-party admin and sales software, not the model: your SaaS vendors are part of your AI attack surface.

Detail & sources

Reports based on a 26 October Washington Post story say Cathedral, a security startup, used AI agents under Anthropic’s bug bounty program to reach sales, HR and IT systems through third-party software. Anthropic confirmed access to a limited set of systems holding business contacts and basic account records. It says its monitoring flagged the activity, it closed access the same day, and it has no evidence of other exploitation. Anthropic disputes Cathedral’s claims about national-security implications and about how much was reached. Reports say Claude conversations, source code and model weights were not accessed. These details come from secondary outlets and are not independently verified.

Anthropic buys Mithril Medicine and names six drug-discovery partners

Why it matters: Frontier labs are moving from selling tools to running drug programs themselves, which changes who competes with pharma R&D.

Detail & sources

Per reports on Anthropic’s 27 October announcement, Mithril was a one-person rare muscular dystrophy startup led by Cody Tranbarger, who used Claude to coordinate work with 11 outside labs and reach mouse testing in under 18 months. Anthropic expects a candidate in human trials as soon as late 2028. Named partners are BigHat Biosciences, Bristol Myers Squibb, Divergence Labs, Enveda, Generate Biomedicines and The Column Group. Most results are preclinical and show no proven patient benefit. This follows Anthropic’s earlier Coefficient Bio deal (background, April 2026; deal value not verified).
