Security14 Oct

Bank of America put 1,000 contractors on flaws that Mythos found

Why it matters: AI-found vulnerabilities are now a budgeted operating cost at big banks, not a lab demo; expect other regulated firms to size similar remediation teams.

Detail & sources

CEO Brian Moynihan told CNBC on 14 October that the contractors were added in the second quarter and are still on site, alongside about 3,000 existing staff. He put spending at “a couple hundred million plus”; one outlet notes that figure also covers anti-money-laundering work, so it is not the cost of Mythos alone. One report says a scan flagged more than 23,000 potential issues, mostly low to moderate severity.

Google launches Universal Cart in the US across Search and Gemini

Why it matters: Agentic shopping is now a shipping product with big-retailer partners, so retail teams must plan for carts, price alerts and checkout that start inside Google.

Detail & sources

The cart tracks saved items, sends price-drop and back-in-stock alerts, and shows promo codes. Users can pay with Google Pay or finish on the retailer’s site; the retailer stays merchant of record. Launch partners include Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta Beauty, Walmart and Wayfair. Reports say YouTube and Gmail were not part of this launch and that Europe is excluded; Google has given no reason.

Policy16 Oct

Massachusetts weighs some of the strictest state AI rules in the US

Why it matters: A state-level audit mandate for frontier labs could become a template that labs must meet outside California and New York.

Detail & sources

The Senate passed its economic development bill on 23 July. It would require independent reviews of catastrophic-risk of frontier models at least every 120 days, published safety frameworks, and attorney-general enforcement. The state could not use findings to halt development. The bill is still in conference. OpenAI wants alignment with Illinois-style annual audits; Anthropic wants stronger evaluations. The governor backs independent safety evaluations, while a business group urges dropping the frequent-testing rules.
