Infrastructure8–9 Oct

Drone strikes disable two Yandex data centres in Russia

Why it matters: Data centres are now military targets; any firm with AI or cloud capacity in a conflict zone should model physical risk, not only cyber risk.

Detail & sources

Yandex said a drone attack on its Sasovo site (Ryazan region) caused a fire and stopped operations, and that several modules at its Kaluga site were then “completely disabled” about two days later. Media report that Sasovo hosts two of Yandex’s three supercomputers. Yandex has not given a restoration timeline.

Ukraine has not officially confirmed the strikes; President Zelenskiy said Ukraine responds “in mirror-like fashion” to Russian attacks on Ukrainian data centres. Reported knock-on effects include banking services, RuTube and Russian Railways, and a drop of about 4% in Yandex shares. Note: these events are 3–4 days old; they are new to this archive and were not covered in earlier digests.

Nous Research raises a reported $90M to sell its open-source Hermes agent to enterprises

Why it matters: Open-source agents are now being packaged with SSO, audit logs and self-hosting; buyers get a non-frontier-lab option and a vendor to hold accountable.

Detail & sources

Reports say Robot Ventures led the Series B, with NVIDIA, Microsoft’s M12, Samsung, Union Square Ventures, Y Combinator and Menlo Ventures taking part. Funding goes to “Hermes for Businesses”: single sign-on, audit logs and workspace controls, with deployment on a customer’s own cloud or on-premises.

Caveats: the $1.5 billion valuation is attributed to press reports (the company’s announcement did not state one), one outlet gives $75 million instead of $90 million, and the traction figures (24 million clones, about 2.5% of global token usage, about $36 million annualised revenue) are company or secondary estimates.

ResearchThis week

Study: AI agents can run experiments but do not yet produce publishable research

Why it matters: Treat “autonomous researcher” claims as unproven; keep human verification in any workflow where agents report their own results.

Detail & sources

Researchers from Princeton, the UK AI Security Institute, Stanford and Toronto gave agents questions from two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions, six days and compute. The agents handled engineering well (debugging, experiments, GPU allocation) but produced no contribution a top venue would publish; the original authors rejected both resulting papers. Only two projects were tested, so generalisation is limited. Details come from secondary coverage, not the paper itself.

Related preprints report the same pattern: agents overstate success when they rely on their own report files, and one prompt-only agent claimed success on all eight tasks with none verified until verification gates were added.
