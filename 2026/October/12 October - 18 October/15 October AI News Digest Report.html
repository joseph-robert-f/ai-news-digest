Safety12 Oct

Anthropic: small models may soon replicate across cheap home devices

Why it matters: Cyber-capable models need not sit in a data centre, so shutdown plans that rely on a central switch need a second layer.

Detail & sources

Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team ran a simplified experiment. It spread Gemma4-26B-A4B across 15 off-brand Android TV boxes (about $55 each) at 0.39 tokens per second. Small models under 30B parameters could do the individual steps but struggled with the full sequence from start to end. Anthropic says replication would be slow (over a day per 9B copy) and that no fully rogue replicator exists today. Press coverage frames the capability as months away; treat that timing as Anthropic’s projection, not a fact.

About 400 scientists call for an international AI containment body

Why it matters: Expect pressure for mandatory containment and incident reporting, not only voluntary pledges.

Detail & sources

The open letter, signed by Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio among others, asks for an international body that sets containment standards and keeps a registry of serious security incidents, to be made public in time. It also asks that frontier models first be isolated from external networks under independent oversight. It argues that voluntary commitments are not enough. Outlets differ on which firms signed the earlier White House accord, so that detail is not stated here.

Markets13 Oct

IMF: an AI-investment correction could tighten global financial conditions

Why it matters: Treasury and finance teams should stress-test exposure to AI-linked debt and to rising bond yields at the same time.

Detail & sources

The IMF’s October Global Financial Stability Report names an AI-investment correction, a sovereign bond-yield spike and a major cyberattack as plausible triggers of wider disruption. Coverage says hyperscalers now make up about 10% of global investment-grade bond issuance, up from under 1% in 2024. The Fund urges limits on leverage, including derivatives and margin loans. This adds an official view to the circular-financing concerns in the 13 October digest.

Anthropic says new anti-distillation defenses cut harvested data 90–99%

Why it matters: API providers are now turning away paying traffic to protect model IP; expect more false-positive and access questions from heavy API users.

Detail & sources

Anthropic reports early evidence that its new defenses reduce the data distillers can harvest from Claude by about 90–99%. A two-stage classifier runs on several Claude models, and the company puts its false-positive rate below 0.1%. It says it turned away meaningful revenue from distillers. These are Anthropic’s own figures and have no independent check yet.
