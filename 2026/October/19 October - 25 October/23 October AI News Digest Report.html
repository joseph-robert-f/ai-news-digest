Security21 Oct

Microsoft: China-linked Storm-0940 used an AI pentest tool to compromise ConnectWise

Why it matters: Attackers can now scale intrusions with off-the-shelf agent tooling, so patch windows and cloud-persistence checks need to be faster.

Detail & sources

Reuters (syndicated) reports the group used ARTEX, an open-source framework that runs any AI model to automate penetration-testing tasks, against hundreds of organizations in defense, finance, government and semiconductors. Most attempts failed, but the group gained privileged cloud persistence at many. A secondary blog citing Microsoft’s report says over 2,250 tenants were reached, sometimes in under 3 hours; I could not verify that figure. Microsoft says the activity did not come from a compromise of its own systems. ConnectWise said on 16 October it was investigating unauthorized access to its Cloud Backup environment.

Developer platforms22 Oct

Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation

Why it matters: The skills format is now vendor-neutral, so teams can invest in it without betting on one model vendor.

Detail & sources

The donation covers the open standard, its name and logo, the GitHub organization and the agentskills.io domain. The AAIF sits under the Linux Foundation, was co-founded by Anthropic, Block and OpenAI, and also hosts MCP. Anthropic says more than 70 agent platforms support skills, with 1 million published and nearly 280 million installs (Anthropic figures). Proposed next steps: versioned releases, a formal proposal process and security guidance for skill authors.

Research15 Oct

Goodfire argues single neurons are more interpretable than assumed

Why it matters: If it holds, safety teams could audit models neuron by neuron, but the result is unreplicated and from a company that sells interpretability.

Detail & sources

Older background, still being debated. In preliminary Qwen3-32B audits, about 90% of neuron poles had one main function, about 7% had two, and 2–4% were unexplained. The companion paper is not out. A critique argues that knowing what a neuron responds to does not explain how neurons combine to compute.
