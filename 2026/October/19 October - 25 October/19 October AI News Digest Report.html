Agents & finance13–19 Oct

Schwab and JPMorgan block outside AI agents from moving client money

Why it matters: Large financial firms are setting the rules for agent access first: read-only data and firm-approved MCP links, not agents that log in as the customer.

Detail & sources

Schwab CEO Rick Wurster told Bloomberg TV the firm blocks unauthorized agents, including Meta’s Muse, and plans MCP servers that allow balance checks and research but not money movement. On the 13 October earnings call, Jamie Dimon said outside agents inside the bank are “too risky,” and said customers who grant access face a “liability transfer.” Some observers say the deeper motive is protecting cash-sweep revenue; that is opinion, not confirmed by the firms. We rely on secondary coverage of the Bloomberg interview.

Markets & legal12–13 Oct

Character.AI files Chapter 11; Disney is the $20M opening bidder

Why it matters: A teen-safety lawsuit and IP-pressure story has become a court-run asset sale, a template for how small consumer-AI firms may exit.

Detail & sources

Character Technologies filed in Delaware on 12 October for a Section 363 sale. Disney’s stalking-horse bid is a perpetual, non-exclusive licence to some IP plus certain claims, not the whole business; rivals can outbid it. The service keeps running during the sale, which is expected to close in late November or early December. Court papers estimate assets and liabilities at $50–100M each. Earlier lawsuits over a teen’s death and an FTC inquiry preceded the filing.

Walmart’s digital shelf labels meet state bills on algorithmic pricing

Why it matters: If pricing algorithms face store-level limits, retailers that use AI for markdowns will need audit trails and plain-language disclosure.

Detail & sources

Walmart plans electronic shelf labels across its U.S. stores within a year, with about half already installed (store counts differ by source). Walmart says prices do not vary by demand, time or shopper and that its 2026 AI patents are “people-led.” Bills to limit dynamic pricing are filed in Maryland, Pennsylvania, Minnesota and New York; the UFCW backs them, and two Democratic senators filed a federal bill. The retail trade group calls the fears hypothetical. We found trade and business outlets, not the AP report some roundups cite.
