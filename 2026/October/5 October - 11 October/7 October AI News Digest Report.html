Mistral previews Large 4, a 1.05T-parameter open-weight model, with weights due 27 October

Why it matters: Europe now has a credible sovereign alternative to US and Chinese frontier models, and the open weights land in three weeks, so procurement and data-residency options change.

Detail & sources

Mistral Large 4 (“Le Chonk”) is a mixture-of-experts model with 1.05T total and 49B active parameters, a 1M-token context window and 160+ languages. It was trained in two months on 4,000 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral’s own European data centres. Public preview is on the Mistral Studio API; Mistral says it is the strongest open-weight model built outside China. That is a vendor claim and independent benchmarks are not yet available.

Anthropic S-1 shows $4.6B revenue, an $8B operating loss and $518B of compute commitments

Why it matters: New detail on the 5 October IPO story: growth is steep but fixed costs are heavy, so the November listing is a bet on continued demand.

Detail & sources

The leaked 261-page prospectus reports 2025 revenue up about 12x to nearly $4.6B and an operating loss of $8B (from $3B). Nearly a quarter of revenue came from two customers, and about 80 pages are risk factors, including “catastrophic or existential” risk language. Compute commitments are at least $518B, roughly 80% non-cancelable. The company targets a valuation of up to $2T, marketing from the week of 9 November. These figures come from a leak, not a final filing.

White House names a Super Intelligence Force led by Jay Clayton

Why it matters: A single federal task force now coordinates AI policy and will consult industry, so expect it to be the main channel for federal asks of AI companies.

Detail & sources

Follow-up to the 4 October brief on Washington’s tightening grip. The task force is led by Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, with FTC chair Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon CTO Emil Michael and OPM director Scott Kupor. It reports to the President and chief of staff Susie Wiles, and will consult consumers, public-interest groups, critical-infrastructure providers and AI companies. It follows last week’s voluntary self-policing accord signed by AI executives.
