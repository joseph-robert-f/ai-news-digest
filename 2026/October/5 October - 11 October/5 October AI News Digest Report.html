LegalThis week

Class action says Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI colluded to slow AI

Why it matters: The industry’s own “pace the frontier” safety push is now an antitrust exposure; expect legal review of any joint safety commitments.

Detail & sources

Reports say consumer plaintiffs allege a Sherman Act Section 1 violation. They point to Dario Amodei’s essay “We Must Pace the Frontier” and the endorsements that followed from Elon Musk, Sam Altman and Demis Hassabis. They seek class certification, an injunction and a declaratory judgment. These are allegations only; no court has ruled.

North Korea says it tested a missile steered by AI; Seoul says it can be intercepted

Why it matters: Claims of AI-guided weapons are mostly unverified, but they raise pressure for export controls and allied missile-defense spending.

Detail & sources

State media said the missile flew more than 700 km toward the East Sea on a low, wave-like path, with AI making real-time course changes. Kim Yo Jong said it could not be avoided. South Korea’s military disputed that, said the missile could have been intercepted, and said its National Security Office met in emergency session and called the launch a UN Security Council violation.

US trust in business use of AI falls to 27%, with the sharpest drop among under-30s

Why it matters: Consumer distrust is a growing brand and regulatory risk for any company shipping AI features, and it feeds the political push for oversight.

Detail & sources

The Bentley-Gallup survey finds 27% of Americans trust businesses to use AI responsibly, down from 31% in 2025. This ends two years of gradual improvement. Among 18–29-year-olds, at least some trust fell from 30% to 20%, and those with no trust rose from 29% to 41%.
