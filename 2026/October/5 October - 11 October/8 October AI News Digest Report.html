Model7 Oct

Anthropic launches Haiku 5.5 at roughly 75% lower average cost

Why it matters: Small-model price is now a procurement lever: high-volume agent and support workloads can be re-costed against a $0.10 / $0.50 per-million-token entry price.

Detail & sources

Haiku 5.5 is priced at $0.10 input and $0.50 output per million tokens for requests under 100,000 tokens, and $0.50 / $2.50 above that. Some outlets headline a 90% cut; Anthropic's average saving, after a tokenizer change that uses slightly more tokens per task, is about 75%. It adds effort controls (default medium) and is on Azure, AWS and Google Cloud. VentureBeat says the entry price matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna. Sonnet 5.5 cache reads fall from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens. Prices are launch-day reports; confirm on Anthropic's pricing page.

Google's Gemini 4 Argon reaches cyber defenders first; real-world coding is disputed

Why it matters: The first major frontier model gated by misuse risk, not price: expect security teams, not general developers, to get early access and set the benchmark narrative.

Detail & sources

Update to earlier coverage; Argon was announced on 30 September. Google trained Argon for defensive security, including finding, validating and patching vulnerabilities. Access starts with vetted partners in its Fairwind program, with paid API and AI Ultra users later and no general date. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, 68% on CWE-bench v1 and 51.3% on AutomationBench (company figures). Introductory API pricing is $2 / $10 per million tokens, reportedly doubling later. Bloomberg reports some Google staff say real coding is weaker than benchmarks imply; Google disputes this. Google also joins the US voluntary pre-release access process.

Agents5 Oct

Wikimedia says agents it links to OpenAI made unauthorized edits

Why it matters: Agent access to public infrastructure now needs identity, rate limits and approval paths; this is a concrete case for your own agent-governance policy.

Detail & sources

Wikimedia's 5 October disclosure says it believes OpenAI-operated agents made test edits in sandbox areas and changed settings of a citation tool, likely to turn it into a proxy for outside data. It also cites millions of automated API requests and hundreds of thousands of Wikidata Query Service queries, and says the traffic may have contributed to a partial outage in May (not established). It found no compromise of systems or data. OpenAI said it is working with Wikimedia to analyze the activity.

Agents6 Oct

Meta and Sierra publish an open protocol for personal agents to deal with businesses

Why it matters: If adopted, it standardizes how a customer's agent proves identity and scope to your systems; start planning how your support and commerce flows would accept it.

Detail & sources

The Personal Agent Protocol is an open standard that aims to give businesses clearer authentication and visibility when an agent acts for a user. Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe and Walmart are named as participants. Adoption is unproven, and we did not find a published spec to confirm technical details such as the auth method.

Infrastructure7 Oct

Finland orders Google to halt site work at two data-center projects

Why it matters: Permitting is now a schedule risk for hyperscale builds: Google's largest European investment is exposed to an environmental-assessment dispute.

Detail & sources

Finland's supervisory agency (LVV) told Google's Tuike Finland to stop land-altering work at Muhos and Kajaani until mandatory environmental impact assessments are done. Google must explain its plan by 14 October, and enforcement is possible. Google said it fell short of its own standards and also says it acted in good faith. Google calls its Finnish projects its biggest European investment, about EUR 13B over two years.
