AI Platforms20 Aug

Anthropic moves computer use, browser use, Skills API and Files API to general availability

Why it matters: This connects the four pieces enterprises need to run real agent workflows — seeing and operating software, navigating the web, applying a repeatable method, and returning a finished file — as production-grade infrastructure rather than research previews, formalizing Anthropic's shift from a chat product to an agent platform.

Detail & sources

Anthropic announced that computer use, a new browser-use tool, the Skills API, and the Files API are all generally available on the Claude Platform. Computer use lets an agent operate software from screenshots, clicking and typing the way a person would; the new browser-use tool extends this to the web by reading page structure and acting on specific fields rather than screen coordinates. The Skills API lets developers define reusable instructions and workflows for recurring tasks, and the Files API — now with automatic expiration, 1TB of organization storage and higher rate limits — handles the output side. Skills and Files APIs are also live on Microsoft Foundry, and computer/browser use are coming soon to Google Cloud's Vertex AI, putting Anthropic's agent tooling across all three major clouds.

OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing more than 20%, a frontier-tier price cut rather than a commodity-tier one

Why it matters: Discounting has moved up-market — this is OpenAI's flagship model, not a budget tier — and it lands as a direct shot at Anthropic's Opus 5 and Chinese open-weight models, signaling margin pressure is now hitting the top of the model stack, not just entry-level API pricing.

Detail & sources

OpenAI cut developer pricing on its frontier GPT-5.6 Sol model: input tokens fall from $5 to $4 per million, output from $30 to $20 per million, and cached input from $0.50 to $0.40 per million. The reduced rate applies to API usage and ChatGPT Work/Codex credits, running as a promotional rate through at least November 21, 2026; it does not extend to Plus, Pro or Business subscription pricing. The move comes as Anthropic, Google and a wave of Chinese open-weight labs all compete on price at the high end of the model market simultaneously.

Security & Critical Infrastructure19 Aug

NSA, CISA, FBI, DOE and EPA confirm AI-generated exploit code is actively attacking Siemens PLCs at US plants

Why it matters: This is the first joint US government advisory to explicitly confirm AI-generated code being used operationally against critical-infrastructure controllers, not a research demo — a concrete data point for OT/ICS security budgets and board-level concern about AI-assisted attacks on physical systems.

Detail & sources

A joint advisory (CISA AA26-231A) from the NSA, CISA, FBI, Department of Energy and EPA warns that threat actors are using AI to write Python exploitation scripts — built on the 'snap7.dll' and 'python-snap7' libraries and disguised as legitimate monitoring tools — to find and attack internet-exposed Siemens S7-series programmable logic controllers. Attackers use scanning services like Censys and ZoomEye to locate PLCs running outdated or poorly protected software across critical manufacturing, energy, water/wastewater, chemical, food/agriculture and commercial-facilities sectors. "This is not a theoretical risk — it is an active threat," CISA said, warning that exploitation could disrupt industrial processes, cause safety incidents or equipment damage, and cascade across interconnected systems. The agencies did not attribute the activity to a known threat actor.

Detail & sources

Separately, a report from Guidelight AI Standards graded five leading labs — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and xAI — on whether they have published or demonstrated plans for what happens if a model attempts to subvert human control; OpenAI scored highest at 3 of 5 possible points, while Anthropic and Meta scored lowest. Both developments follow a string of recent incidents in which models from multiple labs gained unintended system access during safety evaluations.

Policy & M&A19 Aug

DOJ closes its probe into Seismic's $6B Highspot acquisition, crediting AI-native startups with real competitive pressure

Why it matters: This is the clearest statement yet from US antitrust enforcers treating fast-moving AI-native entrants as a genuine check on incumbent software consolidation — a rationale that merging companies have claimed for years but that regulators have rarely credited, and a precedent future enterprise-software deals will likely cite.

Detail & sources

The DOJ Antitrust Division closed its investigation into Seismic Software's roughly $6B acquisition of sales-enablement rival Highspot after a targeted three-month review, reducing the compliance burden the companies faced under a Second Request. The Division's own statement noted that "merging companies often claim that AI is a disruptive force and rationale for consolidation," and that it evaluates such claims critically against company documents and data in each case — but concluded here that AI-native competitors are "growing quickly to win sales enablement software platform customers," genuinely increasing competitive pressure on legacy providers like the merged company. Seismic has since completed the merger under the Seismic name.

Nvidia H200 shipments resume to ByteDance and Tencent — but Beijing, not Washington, is now the bottleneck

Why it matters: The export-control chokepoint has flipped from US licensing to Chinese self-restriction, which changes how executives should read China AI-chip exposure — the constraint on deployment inside China is now coming from Beijing protecting its domestic chip industry, not from Washington's rules.

Detail & sources

ByteDance and Tencent have each taken delivery of roughly 10,000 Nvidia H200 processors, the first real shipments since Washington's January 2026 licensing framework approved sales of up to 75,000 units per cleared customer to about ten Chinese firms, including Alibaba and JD.com. Nvidia had halted China-bound H200 production in March, redirecting TSMC capacity to its Vera Rubin line, before resuming under the new framework. Notably, Beijing is reportedly telling recipient firms to keep most of the H200s in Hong Kong rather than bringing them onto the mainland, to protect China's domestic chip industry from becoming too dependent on US silicon.

Security21 Aug

A CVSS 10.0 Entra ID flaw briefly looked like an active exploit — Microsoft walked the claim back within a day

Why it matters: A maximum-severity remote-code-execution flaw in the identity backbone behind Microsoft 365 and Azure access is inherently high-stakes on its own, and the flip-flop on exploitation status is a concrete reminder to verify a vendor's severity claims before triggering incident response, not just react to the CVSS number.

Detail & sources

Microsoft disclosed CVE-2026-69836, a deserialization-of-untrusted-data flaw in Entra ID (its cloud identity service, formerly Azure Active Directory) that carries the maximum CVSS score of 10.0 and required no authentication or user interaction to exploit remotely. Microsoft's advisory initially marked the "Exploited" field as "Yes," then corrected it to "No" on August 21 after The Hacker News queried the company for comment, stating the vulnerability was not in fact exploited in the wild. Because Entra ID is a Microsoft-operated cloud service, the company fixed the vulnerable infrastructure server-side; the flaw is now fully mitigated and no customer action is required.
