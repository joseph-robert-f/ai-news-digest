Security17–20 Aug

Two widely used open-source AI frameworks are under active attack; CISA's federal patch deadline hits today

Why it matters: Ray and MLflow sit underneath a huge share of production ML pipelines, and both got hit with critical, actively-exploited flaws within days of each other — a reminder that the AI supply chain's infrastructure layer, not just the models, is now a live target, with federal systems on a hard clock to respond.

Detail & sources

CISA added a Ray code-injection flaw (CVE-2025-62593) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 17 and, under Binding Operational Directive 26-04, gave federal civilian agencies just three days — until August 20 — to patch it. The bug lets an attacker bypass Ray's User-Agent-based dashboard protection and, combined with DNS rebinding, achieve remote code execution simply by getting a victim to view a malicious webpage or ad while Ray is running; a fix shipped in version 2.52.0. Separately, an unauthenticated server-side request forgery flaw in MLflow (CVE-2026-64849, CVSS 9.3) affecting versions before 3.15.0 was assigned a CVE on August 17 and was already being scanned and exploited in the wild within hours, according to watchTowr's honeypot network — attackers use a webhook-redirect bypass to reach cloud metadata endpoints and steal credentials and secrets.

Security18 Aug

Microsoft patches "CoSnitch," a one-click Copilot flaw that poisoned its own memory to exfiltrate connected-app data

Why it matters: This is a textbook example of the new agentic attack surface — no malware, no download, just a crafted link that made a trusted AI assistant's own built-in tools (memory, URL fetching, OAuth connections) do the attacking, with the injected instructions persisting across sessions until removed.

Detail & sources

Security firm Varonis disclosed CoSnitch (CVE-2026-24301), a vulnerability chain in Microsoft Copilot Personal that combined an undocumented URL parameter with the standard ?q= query parameter so that opening a single crafted link executed an attacker's prompt instantly — no click or confirmation beyond the initial visit. Once triggered, Copilot could query data from connected OAuth apps such as Gmail, Google Drive and Calendar and quietly relay it to an attacker-controlled server via its own URL-fetching feature, disguising the theft as routine traffic; a memory-poisoning component meant injected instructions could persist across future sessions. Varonis reported the flaw responsibly in December 2025, Microsoft shipped a fix on August 18, and researchers say there is no evidence of exploitation in the wild.

Policy18 Aug

Pennsylvania makes its AI data-center guardrails legally binding — the strictest in the nation

Why it matters: This converts what had been a voluntary incentive program into hard law, forcing developers to internalize the power and water costs of AI buildouts rather than shifting them to ratepayers — a template other states weighing grid strain from data centers are likely to study closely.

Detail & sources

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed an executive order on August 18 making Pennsylvania's "GRID" standards — previously a voluntary path to state support — legally binding on AI data-center developers. Projects now require a Consent Order committing developers to bring and pay for their own power, source an increasing share from clean sources like solar, advanced nuclear and battery storage, hire locally, meet environmental-protection standards and win local approval; all data-center proposals were also pulled from the state's Permit Fast Track program. The order follows stalled legislation in the state Senate to codify the GRID standards after the House passed a bill in June; critics quoted by Pennsylvania Capital-Star argue the order still leaves gaps a future administration could unwind.

Autonomous Vehicles14 Aug

Waymo wins approval to blanket California in robotaxi service across 18 counties

Why it matters: This is the broadest state-level green light any robotaxi operator has received, covering the majority of California's population in one ruling — a strong signal that regulators are moving from city-by-city pilots to statewide frameworks for autonomous ride-hailing.

Detail & sources

The California Public Utilities Commission approved an updated Passenger Safety Plan for Waymo on August 14, clearing it to operate driverless ride-hailing across 18 counties spanning the full San Francisco Bay Area (from Brentwood to Sonoma County's Sea Ranch), an expanded Los Angeles footprint, and new service in Sacramento and San Diego. Waymo says the rollout will be gradual and guided by its existing safety framework, with San Diego service targeted for later this summer; no firm dates were set for the other new markets. The approval follows Waymo surpassing 220 million autonomous rider-only miles and 500,000 weekly paid trips across its existing markets.
