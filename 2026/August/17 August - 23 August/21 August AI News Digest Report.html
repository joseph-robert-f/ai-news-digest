Markets & Infrastructure19 Aug

Google locks in a $12.2B stake option in Marvell, diversifying its custom-chip supply beyond Broadcom

Why it matters: Google is turning a chip-supply relationship into an equity bet, tying its financial upside directly to how much silicon it actually buys — a structure that gives Marvell a captive anchor customer and signals Google no longer wants a single supplier (Broadcom) holding all the leverage on its TPU roadmap.

Detail & sources

Marvell granted Google a warrant to buy up to 58.97 million Marvell shares at $206.58 each — worth $12.18B if fully exercised, which would make Google roughly the fifth-largest Marvell shareholder. Only about 1.4 million of those warrant shares vest in year one; the rest unlock in tranches tied to every $500M in chips Google actually buys from Marvell, covering processors, storage controllers and networking silicon used alongside Google's TPUs. Marvell said the arrangement could generate roughly $120B in revenue through fiscal 2033 if Google hits its purchase targets. Marvell shares jumped nearly 8% on the news, while Broadcom — Google's larger existing custom-chip partner — fell more than 5%.

SK Hynix cancels a record $28.6B in shares as AI memory demand floods its balance sheet

Why it matters: This is the largest treasury-share cancellation in South Korean corporate history, and it's a memory maker — not a chip designer or a hyperscaler — cashing in on the AI boom; it underscores how much of the buildout's economics now run through HBM and DRAM supply, which SK Hynix says it can't produce fast enough even after raising $26B last month.

Detail & sources

SK Hynix's board approved buying back and cancelling 40 trillion won ($28.61B) in treasury shares — about 24.07 million shares, or roughly 3.3% of shares outstanding — with the repurchase window running August 20 through November 19. The company also committed to directing more than 50% of free cash flow generated between 2025 and 2027 to shareholder returns, and separately approved 54 trillion won ($38B) to build two new fabs in South Korea, one for DRAM in Yongin and one for NAND flash in Cheongju. The move followed a share-price slide despite last month's record $26B+ share sale, the largest ever by a non-U.S. company; SK Hynix, SanDisk and Micron shares all rose the day the buyback was announced.

Developer Platforms21 Aug

AI now authors half of all Linear issues — but AI-assisted pull requests merge at half the rate of human code

Why it matters: This is the clearest data yet that agentic coding is genuinely reshaping output — not just hype — but it also quantifies the bottleneck everyone suspected: teams are generating far more code than they can review, and the growing backlog sits squarely on human reviewers, not the agents.

Detail & sources

Linear's own product data shows AI now creates just under half of everything made inside Linear, up from fewer than one in a thousand items two years ago, and at the current growth rate AI will soon author more than humans and integrations combined. Separately, LinearB's 2026 Engineering Benchmarks report, drawn from 8.1 million pull requests across roughly 4,800 teams, found that teams running a connected coding agent roughly tripled weekly PR volume (21 to 65 over two years) versus a modest rise (8 to 10) for teams without one. But 84.5% of manually written PRs merge within 30 days versus just 32.7% of AI-assisted ones; reviewer pickup time has stretched from about 200 minutes to roughly 1,050, and AI-assisted PRs run nearly two and a half times larger (400+ lines at the 75th percentile versus 157 for human-written code). Developer AI adoption overall reached 88.3% in the same report, up from under 72% in early 2024.

Research17–19 Aug

A leak-proof benchmark finds frontier AI recovers published research ideas from bibliographies alone just 3–15% of the time

Why it matters: Most claims about AI "scientific reasoning" are hard to trust because models may simply have memorized the paper being tested; this benchmark is specifically designed so that can't happen, and the resulting scores suggest genuine hypothesis generation — proposing a novel idea from what prior work implies, not retrieving one — remains a real, unsolved ceiling for even the best models.

Detail & sources

"Reconstruction: A Blind Benchmark for Recovering Research Ideas from Pre-Publication Bibliographies," posted to arXiv August 17 and revised August 19, gives models only a paper's pre-publication bibliography (with anonymized references and a temporal citation cutoff) and asks them to propose the paper's actual research idea, which an independent LLM judge then scores against the real, held-out idea. Because the target papers were published after the cutoff, the seed idea provably cannot be in the model's training data, eliminating the retrieval shortcut that undermines most reasoning benchmarks. Solo frontier models scored just 3–15%; a multi-agent Swiss-tournament pipeline that pits candidate hypotheses against each other across rounds reached 42% — better, but still exposing a structural gap between what current models can retrieve versus what they can genuinely originate.

Anthropic and EPFL show self-propagating "mind virus" prompts can spread between AI agents and outlast memory wipes

Why it matters: As agent fleets increasingly hand off state to each other through shared or persistent memory files, this demonstrates a genuinely new propagation vector — one that needs no code execution or network exploit, just an idea an agent is induced to pass along — though the same paper finds a simple, cheap mitigation that works well today.

Detail & sources

Researchers from Anthropic and EPFL (Vassilis Papadopoulos, McNair Shah, Sam Zimmerman and Jack Lindsey) published "Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" on arXiv August 10. Using a simple evolutionary algorithm, they bred prompt payloads that induce an agent to transmit the same idea onward through editable system-prompt or memory files that agent harnesses use to carry state across sessions, and tested spread in two settings: a six-agent simulated coding collaboration, and a chain of paired agents modeled on the open-source OpenClaw (formerly Clawdbot/Moltbot) assistant. Harmful payloads propagated less reliably than benign ones but still sometimes succeeded, and frontier models proved more resistant than older ones; a one-paragraph warning added to an agent's system prompt cut transmission to near zero across every payload tested. The researchers found no evidence of the technique spreading in the wild, and published every tested payload and the code that generated them under an MIT license.

Developer Platforms20 Aug

ChatGPT on Mac can now read, draft and send iMessages, SMS and RCS texts

Why it matters: This hands a general-purpose chatbot direct read/write access to a user's private text-message history and the ability to send messages under a human's name — a meaningful widening of the assistant's reach into personal communication, guarded so far only by a per-message approval prompt that OpenAI itself warns users not to disable.

Detail & sources

OpenAI's new Apple Messages plugin, announced August 20, lets the ChatGPT desktop app on Apple Silicon Macs (not Intel) search and analyze iMessage, SMS and RCS conversation history, summarize threads, draft replies and send messages through Apple's Messages app on the user's behalf. It's available across all ChatGPT plans, including Work and Codex. Outgoing messages require the user to approve the message and its recipient by default; OpenAI's own guidance discourages enabling an optional "persistent approval" setting, warning that it removes the user's last chance to review a message before it sends.

Amazon confirms a sixfold expansion of Prime Air drone delivery to nearly 500 U.S. cities and towns

Why it matters: This moves autonomous last-mile delivery from a handful of pilot metros to national scale in one announcement — the broadest commitment yet from any operator, and a sign the underlying autonomy and airspace-coordination stack has matured enough for Amazon to bet on rapid, wide-footprint rollout rather than another year of city-by-city trials.

Detail & sources

Amazon said Prime Air drone delivery will expand to nearly 500 U.S. cities and towns by the end of 2026, a roughly sixfold increase from its current 11 metro areas across seven states (Arizona, Florida, Kansas, Louisiana, Michigan, Nebraska and Texas), with Chicago, Atlanta, Cleveland, Syracuse and Boise among the next markets to launch. Drones carry packages up to 5 pounds — covering more than 60% of Amazon's most-ordered items — with delivery in as fast as 30 minutes; Prime members get free drone delivery on orders of $50+, with a $2.99 fee below that threshold ($4.99 for non-members). Amazon says it already completes thousands of drone deliveries daily across its existing footprint.
