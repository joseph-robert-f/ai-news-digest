AI research & models
OpenAI × Astra1 Aug

OpenAI unveils Astra by having it solve ten open math problems — including the first explicit non-sofic group

Why it matters: rather than a product launch, OpenAI introduced its next flagship model with a stack of machine-verifiable proofs that mathematicians hadn't cracked in over a decade — a harder-to-fake capability signal than any benchmark, and a sign frontier labs now see original research output, not chat quality, as the next competitive axis.

Detail & sources

OpenAI announced on August 1 that an internal version of Astra, described as its "next major model family," solved ten open problems spanning group theory, von Neumann algebras, high-dimensional geometry, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography, and extremal combinatorics — each unsolved for at least a decade. The headline result is the first-ever explicit construction of a non-sofic group, resolving a question that has stood since Mikhail Gromov introduced the concept of soficity in 1999. Other results include disproving Connes's rigidity conjecture on von Neumann algebras, proving Ehrhart's volume conjecture, resolving three problems from Paul Erdős's catalogue (including problem 183 on multicoloured Ramsey numbers), the first improvement to the general upper bound on high-dimensional sphere-packing density since 1978, a parallel repetition theorem for two-player quantum games, and new lower bounds on the circuit complexity of computing the permanent. OpenAI published a 249-page manuscript alongside machine-checkable Lean 4 certificates for every result on GitHub under an Apache 2.0 license, plus walkthroughs of the model's reasoning; the total compute cost across all ten solutions was roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates. Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers said he would recommend at least one of the proofs for a top journal without hesitation. The disclosure follows 2026 Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman's announcement in July that he is taking leave from the University of Toronto to join OpenAI's AI safety research effort.

EO 14409deadline lapsed 1–2 Aug

White House misses its own August 1 deadline for a frontier-AI review framework — no public deliverables

Why it matters: this is the same deadline covered here yesterday as it arrived; what's new is that it passed with nothing delivered, leaving the labs' pre-release review regime, a classified benchmarking process, and a cross-industry jailbreak-severity standard all in limbo — a concrete signal that federal AI oversight is behind its own self-imposed schedule.

Detail & sources

Executive Order 14409, signed June 2, 2026, required three specific deliverables by August 1: a classified benchmarking process run by the NSA, CISA, and NIST to designate "covered frontier models"; a voluntary frontier-AI disclosure framework managed by Treasury, NSA, CISA, and NIST; and a federal cyber-workforce expansion plan from the Office of Personnel Management. None was made public as the deadline passed. The White House Office of the National Cyber Director had circulated a draft of the disclosure framework to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google roughly two weeks earlier, and the three companies submitted joint revision proposals, but disputes over how to define "covered frontier models" and whether open- and closed-source models should be treated differently remained unresolved. Separately, the TRAINS program, intended to standardize jailbreak-severity scoring across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and xAI, remains without a public status update. The lapse leaves developers and investors without clarity on which models will be designated for the up-to-30-day pre-release federal review the order otherwise makes practically mandatory.

Qualcomm × Modularcompleted 29 Jul

Qualcomm completes Modular acquisition, hands Chris Lattner the keys to its AI software stack

Why it matters: Qualcomm just bought its way into owning the software layer — not just the silicon — for deploying AI workloads from phones to data centers, and put the creator of LLVM and Swift in charge of it, a serious bet against Nvidia's CUDA lock-in on the developer-tooling side of the AI stack.

Detail & sources

Qualcomm completed its acquisition of Modular on July 29, an AI-native software infrastructure company whose platform — including the Mojo programming language, the MAX inference engine, and Modular Cloud — helps developers optimize and deploy generative and agentic AI workloads across different types of computing hardware. Modular co-founder and CEO Chris Lattner, best known for creating LLVM and Apple's Swift language, joins Qualcomm as Executive Vice President of Advanced AI Software and Platforms; Modular's products continue under their existing brands. Qualcomm says the deal is meant to build a unified AI compute platform spanning data center, edge infrastructure, and personal and industrial AI, complementing its existing silicon business with the software layer needed to actually deploy models on it. The deal closed the same week Qualcomm reported Q3 2026 earnings and previewed a broader AI software platform unveiling planned for later in August.

HBM4 × Chip supplyweek of 25 Jul

Nvidia-SK Group and Samsung-Broadcom lock in $950B+ of HBM4 supply through 2030

Why it matters: with high-bandwidth memory sold out industry-wide for all of 2026, these deals aren't just capacity purchases — they're multi-year claims on a scarce input that determine which AI accelerator makers can actually ship at scale, and which get squeezed out for years.

Detail & sources

SK Group and Nvidia signed letters of intent for a partnership worth more than $500 billion spanning AI-factory construction and next-generation memory supply, including SK Telecom building a 2-gigawatt AI factory in Korea on Nvidia's Vera Rubin systems (first phase online in 2027) running on SK hynix's HBM4 memory, plus long-term HBM4 supply and joint development commitments. Separately, Samsung and Broadcom signed an MOU valued at more than $200 billion across memory chips, foundry services, and advanced packaging through 2030, validating Samsung's HBM4 roadmap and its 2-nanometer process. Combined, industry trackers now put new HBM-related commitments announced in the past two weeks at more than $950 billion. The scramble reflects an acute supply crunch: HBM is sold out for all of 2026, trackers estimate a roughly 40% industry-wide shortfall persisting through the end of 2027, and SK Hynix's CEO has warned the crunch could stretch into 2030. The upshot is that Nvidia and Broadcom have effectively locked in HBM4 supply for their AI accelerators years out, while competitors without comparable deals face longer waits for the same chips.
