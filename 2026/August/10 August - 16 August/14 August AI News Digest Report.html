Security13 Aug

China-linked "near-autonomous" AI agents breach Taiwan's nuclear regulator

Why it matters: This is the first documented case of AI agents operating with minimal human oversight against a nuclear safety authority and critical-infrastructure operators — a preview of how offensive AI tooling collapses the time and headcount an intrusion into hardened targets requires.

Detail & sources

Researchers say that over four days in early July, an operator deployed up to eight autonomous AI agents in parallel to compromise 85 Taiwanese government user accounts and exfiltrate 2,500+ personnel records, then pivoted to target Taiwan's nuclear-safety regulator and at least seven major energy companies. Investigators attribute the campaign to China-linked operators with moderate-to-high confidence, citing simplified-Chinese artifacts, and say the agents bypassed model safety guardrails by framing offensive actions as "authorized penetration testing." Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs officially confirmed the attack on August 13.

Models10 Aug

OpenAI ships GPT-5.6-Cyber, an exploit-finding model gated to vetted defenders

Why it matters: OpenAI is now explicitly building and deploying a model tuned for offensive security work — finding zero-days, chaining exploits, bypassing authentication — on the logic that only frontier labs can outrun AI-enabled attackers like the ones now hitting Taiwan. The access-control model it's testing (partner vetting instead of open release) will likely become the template other labs follow for dual-use security models.

Detail & sources

Built on GPT-5.6-Sol with safety training loosened for authorized security work, GPT-5.6-Cyber completed 95% of prompts on OpenAI's internal offensive-security benchmark, versus 1.5% for the unmodified base model and 57.3% for the prior GPT-5.5-Cyber. OpenAI says it used the model to discover CVE-2026-15903, a high-severity V8 (Chrome) vulnerability. Access runs through an expanded Daybreak program: Daybreak Blue offers guardrailed general-purpose models to a wider partner set, while Daybreak Red restricts GPT-5.6-Cyber itself to vetted firms including Accenture, IBM, KPMG, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, and Cloudflare.

Shai-Hulud npm worm resurfaces, spreads through keyv to 400+ packages

Why it matters: A self-propagating worm that mints its own publish credentials via GitHub Actions OIDC and ships with valid provenance attestations is a step up from typical typosquat attacks — and its choice to plant hooks targeting Claude Code and VS Code shows attackers are now specifically going after AI coding-assistant workflows as a foothold.

Detail & sources

On August 4, a compromised maintainer account published a poisoned keyv@6.0.0 carrying a credential stealer and worm logic; JFrog's security research team reports the campaign — a resurgence of the Shai-Hulud malware family — has since spread to 400+ packages across 1,700+ versions, including cache-manager, cacheable-request, and flat-cache, plus downstream packages from unrelated maintainers. The worm scans CI configs, exchanges a minted OIDC token for registry publish rights, and republishes itself with a bumped version, all under legitimate-looking SLSA/provenance attestations. Stolen data includes cloud credentials, registry tokens, GitHub tokens, and database connection strings; researchers found the attacker also committed VS Code and Claude Code hook files designed to fire on checkout, without requiring npm install.

Manus returns to independence as Meta unwinds its $2B acquisition

Why it matters: China's regulator blocked the deal purely on the grounds that Manus's technology and talent originated in China, regardless of its Singapore incorporation — a precedent that puts every other reincorporated Chinese-founded AI startup on notice before a Western acquirer signs a check.

Detail & sources

Meta agreed to buy agentic-AI startup Manus for $2B last December; China's National Development and Reform Commission ordered the deal unwound in April, asserting jurisdiction despite Manus having shut its China offices and reincorporated in Singapore after a Benchmark-led raise. Meta has since cut Manus staff off from its internal systems and barred its own employees from using Manus tools. Manus told users on August 11 it will "soon return to operating as an independent company" and will delete certain user data generated since the December acquisition as part of the separation.

Detail & sources

The Assembly and Senate Appropriations Committees held back-to-back suspense-file hearings on August 13, the mechanism that decides in bulk which bills with fiscal impact advance toward floor votes before the legislature adjourns August 31. Roughly 30 AI-related bills were on the docket, spanning companion-chatbot safety for minors, algorithmic management of gig and warehouse workers, healthcare-AI transparency, copyright protections for creative professionals, and a framework for voluntary frontier-model safety certification.
