SpaceX completes its $60B acquisition of AI coding startup Cursor

Why it matters: Musk now owns one of the largest professional-developer distribution channels in AI coding — plus the code-generation data that comes with it — and is routing it through xAI's Colossus data center, turning a deal announced two months ago into a real capacity and talent play against Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex.

Detail & sources

The deal for Anysphere, maker of the Cursor editor, was announced in June and became effective August 14 at a $60B valuation — the largest acquisition of a venture-backed startup on record. Cursor was generating roughly $2.6B in annualized revenue with rising enterprise sales; under the agreement it will build future products on xAI's Colossus GPU complex in Memphis. The deal hands Musk a large base of professional developers and a stream of code-generation telemetry that could sharpen Grok's coding performance.

Apple trained its own AI model for China, with Alibaba's help

Why it matters: Apple has spent two years leaning on partner models for Apple Intelligence; building a proprietary China-only model, tied to Beijing's registration approval, suggests the company sees no path to running Western models there and is willing to fragment its AI stack by geography to keep iPhone sales compliant with Chinese law.

China's Cyberspace Administration formally registered Apple's generative-AI service in July, clearing a required regulatory step; Apple Intelligence is expected to launch in China in the coming months, giving Chinese users and businesses a materially different AI stack than customers elsewhere.

Models14 Aug

Z.ai holds back GLM-5.3 after it out-hacks Anthropic and OpenAI

Why it matters: An open-weight Chinese model now edges out the two leading U.S. labs' restricted models at finding exploitable vulnerabilities from source code — and Z.ai's response, delaying open release and gating the sharpest capability behind vetted access, is the first time a Chinese lab has voluntarily throttled a frontier open model on offensive-security grounds rather than racing it to release.

Detail & sources

Z.ai's GLM-5.3, announced August 14, scored 84.5% on CyberGym, a benchmark for vulnerability discovery and validation from source code — ahead of Anthropic's restricted Mythos 5 (83.8%) and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol (83.6%). The company says GLM-5.3 already surfaced a serious vulnerability in Cursor. Rather than open-weighting it immediately, Z.ai plans public release roughly two weeks out, pending additional security assessment, and will keep the model's most sensitive cyber functions — exploitation, real intrusion, penetration testing — behind a "trusted access" program that screens requests by intent and task category. Z.ai says its GLM model family has now surfaced 2,400+ security flaws, including 1,000+ rated critical or high.

Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash at half the price, aimed squarely at coding agents

Why it matters: Google is undercutting its own prior Flash pricing by 50% while posting real gains on production-code and long-horizon software-engineering benchmarks — a direct pitch to teams building autonomous coding agents who were previously choosing between Gemini's context window and cheaper rivals.

Detail & sources

Gemini 3.7 Flash ships at $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens, half of 3.6 Flash's list rate, with a 1M-token context window and up to 64K output tokens across text, image, audio, and video. Google says the gains concentrate in software engineering, document-heavy work, and web development: on FrontierCode 1.1 Main, a production-code-quality eval, it scores 43.6% versus 34.4% for 3.6 Flash, and it reaches 65.3% on the long-horizon DeepSWE v1.1 benchmark. The model card describes it as an algorithmic refinement of 3.6 Flash rather than a new pretraining run.

OpenAI previews Ultrafast: GPT-5.6 Sol at 14x speed via Cerebras silicon

Why it matters: Running a frontier reasoning model at 750 tokens/second on dedicated wafer-scale hardware turns inference speed itself into a competitive axis — opening frontier models to latency-critical uses like live voice, incident response, and trading that previously forced a tradeoff between capability and response time.

Detail & sources

OpenAI's new Ultrafast service tier runs GPT-5.6 Sol up to 14x faster than standard processing, generating up to 750 output tokens per second, powered by Cerebras' Wafer-Scale Engine, which keeps model weights on-chip and pipelines tokens across wafers rather than round-tripping through HBM. Ultrafast is in limited preview to a select group of API customers, with OpenAI pitching it for financial research, incident response, customer support, voice applications, and commerce; pricing has not been announced.

Markets14 Aug

DeepSeek hikes V4 API prices up to 11x as its GPU fleet buckles under demand

Why it matters: The lab that triggered a global AI price war eighteen months ago is now rationing access through price instead of undercutting rivals — a concrete signal that even China's most efficient inference operation is capacity-constrained, not just margin-constrained, and a data point for anyone modeling AI cost curves as permanently downward.

Detail & sources

DeepSeek is moving its V4 model family to peak/off-peak billing effective 16:00 UTC on August 16, with some output pricing rising as much as 11x at peak hours (off-peak rates run at half of peak). The company says it is handling roughly 7 trillion tokens of inference per week across a 20,000-GPU fleet, and that demand from the cost-efficient V4 Flash pushed both its East and West data centers to their limits after July 31, culminating in an August 4 outage that appears to have triggered the repricing. DeepSeek also moved V4-Pro to general availability the same week.
