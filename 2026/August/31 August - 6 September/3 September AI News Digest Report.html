AI Safety1 Sep

OpenAI says Astra is the first model to cross its "Critical" cyber-risk threshold

Why it matters: Astra didn't just ace a known-exploit benchmark — it autonomously found two unknown zero-days and chained them into an exploit that broke out of a browser sandbox, the clearest signal yet that frontier models have crossed from theoretical to operational offensive-cyber capability, and a preview of the vetted-access-only model other labs will likely be forced to adopt.

Detail & sources

OpenAI says Astra scored 100% on a known exploit-development benchmark under its Preparedness Framework, then in a separate evaluation autonomously discovered two previously-unknown zero-day vulnerabilities and chained them into a working exploit that escaped its browser sandbox and executed commands on the host — triggered simply by opening a malicious HTML file — plus a second chain that escalated from an unprivileged OS user to root. OpenAI is not broadly releasing Astra's full cyber capability; critical-tier offensive tooling access is restricted to a vetted "Daybreak" defender coalition (Daybreak Blue/Daybreak Red) that includes Akamai, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Fortinet, Oracle, Palo Alto Networks and Zscaler, alongside government and critical-infrastructure testers. OpenAI says it is disclosing the zero-days to the affected maintainers.

Models2 Sep

Google ships Gemini 3.8 Flash — its third Flash release in six weeks

Why it matters: Google is now iterating on its mid-tier model at a cadence that rivals OpenAI and Anthropic's release pace, and it used the same launch to plant a flag in AI-driven vulnerability research — directly overlapping with the offensive-security ground OpenAI's Astra just claimed.

Detail & sources

Gemini 3.8 Flash refines August's 3.7 Flash with less verbose output and gains in software engineering, agentic tasks and multi-step reasoning, at the same introductory pricing ($0.75/$3.75 per million input/output tokens through year-end). It's live now in the Gemini app, AI Studio, Vertex AI/Gemini Enterprise, Android Studio, Antigravity, Search's AI Mode and Google Sheets. Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, a variant tuned for vulnerability discovery, is not broadly available — it's gated behind an invite-only "Fairwind Program" for governments, national cyber authorities and critical-infrastructure operators.

Security1 Sep

CrowdStrike pairs offense and defense AI models, built on Nvidia's open Nemotron line

Why it matters: An autonomous red-team model probing a live digital twin of a customer's environment while a defense model remediates in a closed loop is a concrete step beyond "AI writes a report" security tooling — and a validation of Nvidia's push to get its open Nemotron models embedded in enterprise security stacks.

Detail & sources

Unveiled at Fal.Con, CrowdStrike's SafeMind pairs an offensive model ("Red Tempest," which emulates adversary techniques) with a defensive model ("Blue Solano"), both fine-tuned from Nvidia's open Nemotron models using CrowdStrike's own threat telemetry and 15 years of incident-response data. Red Tempest probes a digital twin of a customer's environment built with Nvidia simulation technology; Blue Solano remediates, and the cycle repeats inside Falcon.

Dev Platforms1 Sep

Perplexity splits agent tasks between the cloud and local Mac models to keep sensitive files private

Why it matters: "Hybrid Compute" is a concrete, shipped answer to the objection that's dogged agentic AI all year — that letting an agent touch your files means sending them to the cloud — and it comes free, pressuring other agent vendors to match it rather than charge a privacy premium.

Detail & sources

Perplexity's Computer agent now routes tasks between frontier cloud models and local models running on Apple silicon (macOS 15+, 24GB+ unified memory), gated by an on-device privacy classifier that keeps sensitive files — tax returns, medical records, litigation documents — off the network entirely. It ships with three local models (Gemma 4 E4B, Qwen3.6 35B-A3B, and an in-house Perplexity model); locally-run tasks are free. Perplexity also open-sourced the on-device PII classifier, built with the Secure Intelligence Institute.

Enterprise2 Sep

McKinsey: a third of enterprises now skip software purchases in favor of building with AI agents

Why it matters: This is the clearest survey data yet that agentic coding tools are cutting into enterprise software spend, not just developer productivity — but the same survey shows enterprise-level EBIT impact still flat, so the build-vs-buy shift is running well ahead of proof it pays off.

Detail & sources

McKinsey's State of AI 2026 survey (1,719 respondents, 97 countries, fielded May–June) found 32% of organizations have skipped at least one software purchase because they could build the equivalent with agentic coding tools, and the share of large enterprises scaling agents in at least one business function rose from 27% to 40%. Yet only 37% of respondents report any enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI, versus 80% reporting individual productivity gains, and one in five organizations now cite AI operating costs as a real constraint.

AIR raises $50M to vet the skills and plug-ins AI agents pull into enterprise environments

Why it matters: As agents increasingly install their own tools, skills and MCP servers at runtime, software supply-chain risk shifts from "what did a developer import" to "what did an autonomous agent decide to fetch" — this raise is an early bet that governing that becomes its own security category.
