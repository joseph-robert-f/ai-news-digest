Security29-30 Aug

Infostealer malware is hijacking live Claude sessions to burn through paid usage

Why it matters: because the malware steals already-authenticated session cookies rather than passwords, it bypasses 2FA and SSO entirely — a reminder that account security for AI tools now hinges on endpoint hygiene, not just credential strength.

Detail & sources

Anthropic said it has identified Vidar, LummaC2, StealC, RedLine and Acreed infections on Windows plus Atomic Stealer on a small number of Macs replaying stolen Claude sessions to consume victims' usage limits. The company is signing affected users out, wiping saved payment methods, and refunding unauthorized charges — but warns that signing out doesn't remove the malware itself, so a still-infected machine can have its next session stolen the same way.

Research30 Aug

NPR/NewsGuard test: AI chatbots debunk foreign propaganda more reliably than search engines

Why it matters: chatbots correctly rebutted roughly three-quarters of false narratives from China, Iran and Russia, outperforming Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo and Yandex — but AI-generated summaries sitting on top of search results fared markedly worse, suggesting the safety gap is architectural, not just model quality.

Detail & sources

NewsGuard researchers built 30 test questions from false narratives that first circulated between December 2025 and July 2026, then ran them against major chatbots and search engines, scoring responses against NewsGuard's own fact-check database. The finding complicates the narrative that generative AI is inherently worse than search for spreading disinformation — direct chatbot answers held up better than AI Overviews layered onto search.

Research27 Aug

MIT framework pushes protein design past sequences that already exist in nature

Why it matters: most generative protein models still lean on sequences resembling natural proteins even when asked to design something new; folding physical stability principles directly into the model raises the odds a truly novel, non-natural sequence will actually fold and function — a meaningful unlock for drug and enzyme design pipelines.

Detail & sources

The MIT Biology framework, PottsMPNN, incorporates the physics governing protein structure and stability to better predict how mutations affect stability and to generate structurally feasible sequences that don't resemble any known native protein. The work was published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.
