Policy & Law28 Aug

Federal judge rules the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic was "illegal and baseless"

Why it matters: This is the first time a court has struck down a government attempt to punish an AI lab for refusing a military use case — a precedent that limits how far agencies can lean on supply-chain-risk designations to retaliate against companies that draw their own safety lines.

Detail & sources

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin issued a 59-page order on August 28 blocking the Department of Defense's designation of Anthropic as a "supply chain risk," a status that had cut the company off from certain military contracts. The designation, ordered by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, followed Anthropic's refusal to let the Pentagon use Claude for domestic surveillance or autonomous-weapons applications; it marked the first time a U.S. company had been publicly designated a supply-chain risk under the procurement statute, which is intended to guard against foreign sabotage of military systems. Anthropic sued in March, arguing the move violated its First Amendment right to free speech and its Fifth Amendment right to due process, since it was never given a chance to contest the designation. Judge Lin agreed, writing that "the empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics," and finding the Pentagon acted with a "desire to make a public example" of Anthropic. The government is expected to appeal.

OpenAI moves to cut off Cursor's model access after SpaceX completes its $60B acquisition

Why it matters: A huge slice of Cursor's user base builds on OpenAI models by default, and OpenAI just signaled it will pull that access in under three months — forcing an accelerated migration for developers and turning the Musk-Altman rivalry into a direct disruption of a widely used coding tool.

Detail & sources

OpenAI announced on August 28 that it is ending its model-supply partnership with Cursor (maker Anysphere) following SpaceX's completed acquisition of the company this month, a deal first announced in April at a $60B all-stock valuation. Under OpenAI's proposal, Cursor's direct access to OpenAI's models would end November 12. OpenAI said its contract allows it to cancel the partnership within a limited window after a change of control, and that it "cannot be confident" SpaceX will use its technology within OpenAI's terms of service, citing its own experience with Musk-controlled companies — including Twitter/X and xAI — violating past agreements. OpenAI said it recognizes the decision most directly affects developers who rely on its models inside Cursor and that it is working through the transition.

Nvidia pauses its AI-cloud revenue-sharing financing program amid internal antitrust concerns

Why it matters: Nvidia's own staff flagged that a financing structure giving it 50% of upside revenue and a say over which customers could use rented capacity looked too much like market control — a sign that even Nvidia is treading carefully around how deep its leverage over the AI-cloud supply chain can go before regulators take notice.

Detail & sources

The Wall Street Journal reported that Nvidia has paused some deals under its AI Compute Partnership, a credit program launched less than two months ago to help smaller "neocloud" AI-cloud providers finance GPU purchases. Under the structure, Nvidia agreed to rent back computing capacity a provider couldn't sell itself, splitting revenue above an agreed hourly rate roughly 50/50. Some Nvidia employees warned the arrangement could draw antitrust scrutiny, and early partners objected to the extent of control Nvidia sought, including restrictions on which end customers could use the capacity. Nvidia is said to have stepped back from the initiative last week and could revamp or fold it into another program later; the company has publicly denied fully pausing its cloud-commitments efforts.

Privacy & Consumer AI28 Aug

Meta closes the loophole letting people cover the LED to keep secretly recording on its AI glasses

Why it matters: The capture-indicator light is the only cue bystanders get that Meta's smart glasses are recording, and this is Meta's second privacy patch in under two months — a sign the company is playing catch-up on a hardware design flaw only after repeated public reports of people using the glasses to record others covertly.

Detail & sources

Meta is rolling out a software update, reported August 28, that stops an active video recording on its AI-powered smart glasses if the wearer covers the front-facing capture LED mid-recording. Previously, the glasses blocked filming if the light was already obstructed before a recording started, but did nothing if someone began recording first and covered the light afterward — a gap that let wearers discreetly keep recording despite the indicator being designed as a bystander-consent cue. It's the second privacy-focused firmware change in under two months, following an earlier update that disables the camera if the LED itself is tampered with or destroyed; Meta has also launched a public-education push explaining what the capture LED means.

Security25 Aug

Researchers show invisible HTML text can silently hijack an AI email summarizer's output

Why it matters: As inboxes fill with AI-generated summaries that people read instead of the underlying email, an attacker only needs to hide a few hundred characters of zero-size, white-on-white text to rewrite what the reader believes the email said — with no visual sign of tampering in the client itself.

Detail & sources

Forcepoint's X-Labs research team published a proof-of-concept on August 25 demonstrating an indirect prompt-injection attack against AI email summarizers. The team embedded hidden instructions in an email using HTML styled with zero font size and white-on-white color, invisible in a client like Outlook but fully present in the raw content passed to the summarization model; the visible email held 537 characters, while 1,009 characters — including 472 characters of hidden injection text — were sent to the LLM. In the test, the hidden payload successfully altered the summarizer's output, changing an invoice deadline and deleting information, without any visible indication to the reader that the message had been tampered with. Forcepoint says the issue isn't specific to any one LLM provider or summarizer, but reflects a general risk in feeding untrusted email content directly to a model without sanitization.
