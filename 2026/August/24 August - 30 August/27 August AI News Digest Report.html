Nvidia posts a record $96.2B quarter, but Wall Street reads the fine print instead of the top line

Why it matters: Nvidia beat on revenue and data-center sales again, but the number that moved analysts wasn't the top line — it was supply commitments more than doubling quarter-over-quarter, driven largely by memory procurement, while the stock traded roughly flat despite the beat. The market is starting to price the memory and supply-chain squeeze as the real constraint on AI capex, not GPU demand.

Detail & sources

Nvidia's Q2 fiscal 2027 results (quarter ended July 26): revenue of $96.2B, up 18% sequentially and 106% year-over-year; GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins of 75.0%; GAAP EPS of $2.46 and non-GAAP EPS of $2.22. Data center revenue hit $89B, above the $86.33B analyst estimate and up 117% year-over-year, now 92% of total sales. Guidance for the current quarter came in at roughly $108B, plus or minus 2%. The standout line item was supply commitments, which jumped from $119B last quarter to $279B, which Nvidia said was "primarily related to the procurement of memory." Shares were down roughly 1% despite the beat.

Nvidia reportedly agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

Why it matters: If it closes, Nvidia would own the default distribution hub for open-weight models and datasets that rival chipmakers and labs treat as neutral infrastructure — extending its grip from the compute layer into the model-distribution layer at nearly 3x Hugging Face's last funding valuation.

Detail & sources

The Information first reported Wednesday night that Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9B, citing a person familiar with the matter; CNBC, TechCrunch and other outlets have corroborated the report, but neither Nvidia nor Hugging Face has confirmed a signed agreement, and talks could still fall apart. The deal follows earlier reporting over the weekend of August 22–23 that Hugging Face was fielding buyout interest at $13B+. Hugging Face last raised money in 2023 at a $4.5B valuation in a round led by Salesforce Ventures, and had earlier this year turned down a $500M Nvidia investment at a $7B valuation, saying it didn't want a single dominant investor.

Policy & Regulation26 Aug

Meta agrees to pay $16.68B and rebuild teen guardrails to end a 29-state addiction lawsuit

Why it matters: One of the largest consumer-tech settlements ever locks in specific, enforceable product changes — mandatory overnight access blocks, default daily time caps, blocked notifications during school hours — turning what had been voluntary "well-being" features into contractual obligations, disclosed mid-trial with Instagram's own chief already having testified.

Detail & sources

The settlement, disclosed in a court filing on August 26 during an ongoing federal trial in Oakland, resolves claims from 29 states that Meta engineered Facebook and Instagram to be addictive to children, deceived the public about platform safety, and unlawfully collected data from minors. Required changes include blocking teen access between midnight and 6 a.m., a default two-hour daily time limit, blocking notifications during school hours, prompts after every 15 minutes of continuous use, disabling like-counts and cosmetic-surgery/makeup filters for teen accounts, stronger age verification, and expanded parental controls. Meta denied wrongdoing in agreeing to settle.

Meta preps a $199.99/month consumer AI agent, betting subscriptions can outrun ad revenue

Why it matters: Meta is the last of the big consumer platforms to commit to a paid, task-completing agent tier — testing whether people will pay premium prices for an agent that books, orders and files tasks across third-party services, just as the company absorbs a $16.68B settlement bill and looks for AI-driven revenue beyond advertising.

Detail & sources

Hatch, the consumer-facing version of Meta's internal "OpenClaw" agent, could launch as early as late August or early September, according to internal documents reviewed by The Information. It's designed to complete tasks across services such as DoorDash, Etsy, Reddit, Yelp and Microsoft Outlook, and Meta has considered a premium subscription tier priced as high as $199.99/month for higher usage limits. A new in-house model codenamed "Watermelon" is expected to follow in October as part of the same monetization push.

Security26 Aug

CISA's latest must-patch list pairs a new Citrix NetScaler flaw with five vulnerabilities as old as 2015

Why it matters: A fresh, actively-exploited Citrix NetScaler bug demands patching by August 29, but the unusual inclusion of five long-dormant CVEs is the more important signal — attackers are still succeeding against legacy, unpatched infrastructure that many teams assumed was no longer a live risk.

Detail & sources

CISA added six CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 26. The new one, CVE-2026-8452, is a pre-authentication heap-memory overflow in NetScaler ADC/Gateway's SAML/AAA handling; researchers have already demonstrated a path to pre-auth remote code execution following public proof-of-concept code, with attackers observed dropping web shells. The other five are older bugs resurfacing as actively exploited: CVE-2015-3246 (Red Hat libuser race condition), CVE-2015-5287 (Red Hat ABRT privilege escalation), CVE-2019-1068 (Microsoft SQL Server RCE), CVE-2021-23758 (Ajax.NET Professional deserialization) and CVE-2022-0995 (Linux kernel out-of-bounds write). Federal agencies must remediate the NetScaler flaw by August 29.

AI Labs26 Aug

Altman tells Time OpenAI expects an internal "AGI" system by year-end, built around Astra

Why it matters: This is OpenAI's clearest public timeline commitment yet on AGI, tied to a concrete internal system rather than a vague roadmap — and it lands alongside a visible strategic narrowing, with Sora, the Disney partnership and the Atlas browser being wound down ahead of a targeted Q4 IPO.

Detail & sources

In a Time interview published August 26, Sam Altman said OpenAI would have an internal system by the end of 2026 that meets his definition of AGI — a highly autonomous system that outperforms humans at most economically valuable work — while acknowledging OpenAI is "not quite yet" there; chief research officer Mark Chen put the company at "80% of the way." The vehicle for that claim is Astra, described internally as an automated AI research intern capable of coordinating multiple agents to work a research-math problem for days and, in demos, of navigating desktop software to complete work across applications. Altman briefed lawmakers in Washington on Astra's capabilities days before the interview ran. OpenAI is winding down Sora, its Disney content partnership and the Atlas browser to refocus resources, and is targeting a fourth-quarter 2026 IPO.
