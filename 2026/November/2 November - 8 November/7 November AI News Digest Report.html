Enterprise2 Nov (reported)

Goldman Sachs pulls Claude Code and Copilot from bankers and traders

Why it matters: one of the most AI-forward banks is narrowing agentic coding tools to about 12,000 engineers on productivity and risk grounds. Expect other regulated firms to review who gets coding agents.

Detail & sources

Bloomberg reported on 2 November that Goldman removed the tools from roughly 10,000 non-engineers in October, after a period when staff were encouraged to build prototypes (“vibe coding”). Its in-house assistant stays widely available. An unconfirmed single-source report says Apple cut some engineers' daily AI spend caps; we did not include it as a story.

OpenAI reportedly launches GPT-6 Cyber with gated access and higher prices

Why it matters: cyber-capable models are now sold as vetted-access products, priced above rivals. Security teams must now compare vendors on gated access, not only on public benchmarks.

VentureBeat reports the model finds and exploits software vulnerabilities, needs approval for access, and costs more per API token than Anthropic and Google equivalents. We could not open VentureBeat's page in this session, so we rely on its headline and search summary. Treat details as unverified until OpenAI's own post is checked.

Policy30 Oct (background)

Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic back one robots.txt opt-out for AI training

Why it matters: publishers get one signal instead of many bot rules, but it does not reach data already used, and rollout is slow.

Detail & sources

The shared token is __usage_ai-training. Sources disagree on who has committed: most list all four, some list three. Google and Microsoft are reported to start support in 2027. AI Overviews and AI Mode keep a separate Google control. The standard does not remove content from existing models.

  • The Next Web: one AI training opt-out
  • Search Engine Journal: shared AI training opt-out
  • How2Shout: sites can soon refuse AI training with one rule
Models28 Oct (background)

Google previews Gemini 4 Carbon, limited to vetted cyber defenders

Why it matters: the top Gemini model is not for general sale. Google and OpenAI now both stage their strongest models through security-first programmes.

Detail & sources

Carbon is available first through Google's Fairwind Program, not in the Gemini app or public API, with no date for wider release. Gemini 4 Argon remains the generally available model. Google joins a voluntary US-government pre-release test. Reported prices conflict ($2/$10 vs $4/$20 per million tokens), and the benchmark figures are Google's own.

Models26 Oct (background)

GPT-6.1 Terra halves the price of OpenAI's computer-use model

Why it matters: at $1/$5 per million tokens, agent workloads that were too costly last quarter may now pay back. OpenAI itself points the hardest coding work to Sol or Astra.

Detail & sources

OpenAI says Terra is about three times faster on computer-use tasks and cuts error rates on its own hallucination-prone test from 53.6% to 16.7%. Vals.ai ranks it #10 of 28 on its index at 60.17%. One comparison puts Claude Opus 5.5 slightly ahead at higher cost per task. Benchmarks are mostly vendor claims.
