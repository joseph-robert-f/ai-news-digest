Policy · Chips19 Nov

House panel publishes licences: about 879,000 Nvidia and AMD chips approved for China

Why it matters: The approvals are far larger than the shipments seen so far, so export-control rules and congressional pressure matter more to China-facing revenue than headline licence counts do.

Detail & sources

The House Foreign Affairs Committee, chaired by Brian Mast, released Commerce Department licence records. They show approvals covering 879,000 Nvidia and AMD chips for Chinese firms between December 2025 and May 2026, including 75,000 for Alibaba, 150,000 for Tencent, 43,000 for Baidu and 4,000 for BYD. Companies had sought about 3.4 million. Some approvals covered firms the Pentagon lists as Chinese military companies. Nvidia said the licences carried strict conditions and limited volumes, and that “despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China.” What is new: the 21 November digest covered Nvidia ending licensed Hopper shipments; this item adds the scale of what Commerce had approved. The committee’s own numbers come via press reports; we did not see the primary release, so treat exact totals as reported figures.

Product · Agents19 Nov

Gemini for Mac can now operate desktop apps and websites for you

Why it matters: Google now matches OpenAI and Anthropic on desktop agents, which moves the competition to permissions, auditability and which tasks IT will allow an agent to run.

Detail & sources

Google began rolling out computer use in the Gemini Mac app on 19 November. Users add “@Computer use” to a prompt and Gemini clicks, types and fills in forms in an isolated background window. It is limited to US English, personal accounts and Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers on Apple-silicon Macs. Users pick which apps Gemini can reach, can stop it at any time, and approve sensitive actions first. Google’s post does not say which model powers it.

Regulation · Devices19 Nov

Dutch privacy regulator tells consumers not to buy AI camera glasses

Why it matters: A regulator citing a hallucinated emergency call, not only privacy, is a new kind of product-safety argument that could shape rules for AI wearables in the EU.

Detail & sources

The Dutch data protection authority (AP) tested six camera-equipped AI glasses. In a simulated resuscitation, one pair said it had called 112 and that an ambulance was coming, which the AP called a hallucination. It also cited hidden recording and hacking risk, and repeated its call for bans in hospitals, courts and places of worship. The advice is not binding, and the AP did not name the six models. Related background: consumer groups sued Meta in October over its glasses, with a preliminary hearing on 26 November, and two Dutch retailers paused sales.

Infrastructure · Climate19 Nov

Meta’s emissions rose 45% and it is “re-evaluating” its 2030 net-zero goal

Why it matters: AI capacity growth is now colliding with public climate pledges at a hyperscaler, so expect more gas-backed power deals and tougher questions on data-centre permits.

Detail & sources

Meta’s 2026 sustainability report shows greenhouse gas emissions up 45% and electricity use up 35%. Executives said they are reviewing timelines and milestones for the 2030 net-zero goal, and gave no replacement target. Meta backs new gas capacity, including seven proposed Entergy units in Louisiana (over 5,200 MW) and a 200 MW plant approved in Ohio. Water use fell 4% overall but rose 24% in highly water-stressed regions.
