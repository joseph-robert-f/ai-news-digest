Chips & Leadership23 Nov

Nvidia’s board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, effective immediately

Why it matters: The world’s most valuable AI chip supplier has a settled CEO again, but an independent investigation is still open — expect governance and China-policy questions to continue.

Detail & sources

Nvidia says the board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as President, CEO and director after talks with him, and that it received input from senior US government officials. Colette Kress, interim CEO since the 12 Nov resignation, remains CFO. An independent-director committee will finish its investigation. This is a material change from 17–18 Nov, when chair Harvey Jones said the board had no plan to reinstate him. Press accounts of the reasons for the resignation differ, and only the company statements are used here.

Markets & Risk13 Nov (background)

Fed survey: AI is now the most-cited risk to the financial system

Why it matters: Regulators’ contacts now see AI-linked valuations as a bigger threat than a broad market correction — boards should expect more questions on AI exposure.

Detail & sources

In the Fed’s 13 Nov Financial Stability Report, 87% of surveyed market contacts named AI as a risk, up from 50% in spring. The sample was small (23 contacts) and measures how often a risk was named, not its probability. A risk-asset correction was named by 52%.

Funding19 Nov (follow-up)

Anthropic’s IPO becomes the test case for circular AI financing

Why it matters: If the supplier-investor loop works as a liquid public market, expect copycat structures; if not, AI infrastructure financing gets harder to price.

Detail & sources

TechCrunch says Amazon and Google hold stakes worth about $307.9B combined at the $83 IPO price, and that Anthropic expects about $220B in cash after the IPO and a $26B private placement. Fortune, citing the prospectus, reports a Broadcom backstop on a $63B equipment-financing arrangement and a filing warning that compute expansion depends on a few partners and financiers. These figures come from press accounts of the prospectus, not the SEC filing itself. The listing itself was covered on 23 Nov.

Anthropic: more internal PRs pass after one AI review-and-fix round

Why it matters: AI review is becoming a throughput tool, not just a safety net — but humans still own the approval decision.

Detail & sources

InfoQ reports that, per Anthropic, the share of internal pull requests passing after one review and fix round rose from 65% to 78%. Only InfoQ was found carrying these numbers. Anthropic’s Code Review (launched March 2026 for Team and Enterprise) does not approve pull requests; that stays with human reviewers. It was priced at roughly $15–25 per review at launch, per TechCrunch.
