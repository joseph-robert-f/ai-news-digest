Engineering25 Nov

Anthropic rewrote its Messages API in Rust, using Claude agents to do most of the work

Why it matters: a 70% CPU and 80% memory cut on the service that serves Claude is a real cost lever. The lesson is in the method: verification, not code generation, was the hard part.

Detail & sources

Anthropic's engineering post says a partial Rust component called from Python gave only 8–10% CPU savings, because each reply crossed the language boundary three times. A full rewrite followed. An early build passed about 98% of tests but matched only about 25% of production-like inputs, so the team diffed old and new services on live traffic until behaviour matched 99.6% before cutover. Public traffic moved in September without downtime. Median time to first token fell about 20% for Claude Code requests. These are Anthropic's own figures, reported by several outlets.

Research24 Nov

OpenAI preprint claims the matrix-multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

Why it matters: if it holds, it settles a decades-old open problem and shows AI-assisted maths reaching core computer science. It is unreviewed, so treat it as a claim, not a result.

Detail & sources

The 111-page preprint, credited to “OpenAI” and dated 24 November, claims the exponent equals 9/4 over every field. An earlier 2 October OpenAI preprint showed 9/4 is reachable over the complex numbers. Coverage notes the lower bound covers division-free circuits, the strongest claim is not machine-checked, and the result is asymptotic with no practical speed-up. An independent arXiv exposition of the upper bound exists.

PolicyOngoing · rule of 16 Nov

US remote-access chip rule starts to bite: sellers offload GPUs

Why it matters: renting compute abroad is no longer a clean workaround for China-linked firms, so plan for licence checks, KYC and more supply in the secondary market.

Detail & sources

Background, 16 November: Commerce's BIS issued an interim final rule requiring licences for data centres supplying more than 12 million aggregate TPP to firms in China or other D:5 countries. Existing operators have 90 days to get a licence. The rule adds licensing for advanced chips shipped to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil. New: by 25 November, reports say chip sellers who supplied overseas facilities are offloading GPUs, and Chinese firms are seeking other routes to Nvidia compute.
