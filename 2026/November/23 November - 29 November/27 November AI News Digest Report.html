Markets25 Nov

S&P puts Nvidia’s AA rating on CreditWatch over governance

Why it matters: The risk is leadership and board structure, not earnings; a one-notch cut would raise Nvidia’s borrowing costs and tests how much AI-supply-chain concentration lenders will accept.

Detail & sources

S&P cited board independence, weak succession planning and “key man” risk after Jensen Huang’s brief ouster and reinstatement (covered 24 Nov). It expects to finish the review within 90 days; any cut would be AA to AA-. Moody’s cut its outlook to stable from positive on 18 Nov. No downgrade has happened yet.

Infrastructure25 Nov

FCC approves SpaceX’s plan for up to one million orbital data-center satellites

Why it matters: Regulators are now treating space-based compute as a real power-supply option, but deployment is years away and the licence does not require all one million to launch.

Detail & sources

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr announced approval of the “Starmind” constellation. Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society opposed it. Per Newsweek, deployment must start by November 2033 with 10% by 2035; SpaceX’s IPO filing points to first compute satellites as early as 2028. Outlets differ on some detail, so treat the schedule as reported, not final.

  • CBS News: SpaceX gets FCC approval
  • NBC News: green light for 1 million satellites
  • Newsweek: deadlines and China context

OpenAI starts alpha tests of Private Inference for business prompts

Why it matters: If it works, regulated buyers could use frontier models on sensitive data without trusting the vendor’s staff; today it is an alpha with no price or date.

Detail & sources

Sam Altman announced the test on X. Prompts and replies run in hardware-isolated confidential-computing environments that OpenAI says can be independently verified. It is the second part of OpenAI’s “Private Intelligence” package, after Private Safety Processing. Coverage is secondary; OpenAI’s product page is the primary reference.

Policy24 Nov

New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years

Why it matters: Consumer AI hardware now faces a state-level moratorium; any firm shipping talking AI toys must plan for a New York exit by February 2027.

Detail & sources

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed the bill on 24 Nov. It bars making, distributing or selling toys with embedded AI chatbots. Penalties reach $15,000 per day per violation, enforced by the Attorney General. It takes effect 90 days after signing. One outlet gives 22 Feb 2027; check the bill text. It follows an October law limiting chatbot features for minors.

  • Fox 5 NY: 5-year ban and 90-day delay
