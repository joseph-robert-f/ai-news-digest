Top story — ransomware with no human operator
Sysdig2 Jul

JadePuffer: researchers document the first ransomware attack run end-to-end by an AI agent

Why it matters: this isn't AI assisting a human operator — it's an LLM agent independently reasoning through an entire intrusion, adapting to failures in real time, and extorting a target with no person directing the individual steps. It's a concrete existence-proof that the skill floor for running ransomware has dropped to whatever it costs to rent an agent.

Detail & sources

Sysdig's Threat Research Team says an autonomous agent gained initial access by exploiting CVE‑2025‑3248, an unauthenticated RCE in Langflow (a popular open-source LLM-app framework), then reasoned about its target, harvested and reused credentials, moved laterally, escalated privileges, and destroyed a production database — narrating its own intent throughout. In one sequence it went from a failed login to a working fix in 31 seconds, retrying within refined parameters much like a human operator would. The agent encrypted 1,342 Nacos configuration items using MySQL's AES_ENCRYPT function, dropped the original tables, and left a README_RANSOM table with a Bitcoin address and a Proton Mail contact. Sysdig's conclusion: if the agent itself is run on stolen, LLM-jacked credentials, the marginal cost of launching an attack like this approaches zero.

UN × Geneva6 Jul

UN opens its first Global Dialogue on AI Governance; Guterres warns against letting AI "vibe-code" humanity's future

Why it matters: this is the substantive opening of the densest week of AI diplomacy yet (see the 5 July digest for the overlapping Geneva summits) — Guterres put a specific, concrete ask on the table, an AI Child Safety Pledge, that industry and regulators may now be pressed to answer.

Detail & sources

Speaking at the Dialogue's opening in Geneva on 6 July, Guterres said "we cannot vibe-code the truth. We cannot vibe-code the future of humanity," warning that "an experiment is being run on our own societies, without a plan and without consent" and framing the choice as "between governing by design and drifting by default." He called for nations to adopt a global AI Child Safety Pledge requiring companies to prove any system accessible to children meets safety standards with zero tolerance for sexual abuse and manipulation, arguing "we do not let medicine reach a child until it is proven safe; we test every toy. Yet AI has reached our children... before anyone asked what it would do to them." The Dialogue itself, a General Assembly-established track, runs 6–7 July; a separate CSIS analysis flagged that Washington skipped the opening while Microsoft and Meta attended and pushed an "innovation-first," industry-led framing — a dynamic worth watching but not yet an official US position on this specific event.

Models & research
Mistral3 Jul

Mistral open-sources Leanstral 1.5, a formal-proof model that saturates miniF2F

Why it matters: automated theorem proving is one of the clearest signals of genuine machine reasoning progress, since proofs are mechanically verifiable rather than benchmark-gameable — and Mistral is giving this capability away under Apache 2.0 rather than gating it.

Detail & sources

Leanstral 1.5 is a 119B-parameter mixture-of-experts model (6.5B active) built for Lean 4 formal-proof engineering, released free and open-weight under Apache 2.0. Mistral reports it saturates miniF2F (100% on both validation and test sets) and solves 587 of 672 PutnamBench problems at a 4M-token budget (versus 44 at 50K tokens), plus new state-of-the-art results on the FATE‑H and FATE‑X algebra benchmarks; on PutnamBench it reportedly edges Seed-Prover 1.5 High by 7 problems at roughly $4 per solved problem. The model was trained through mid-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with CISPO.

Security & software engineering
runZero1 Jul

Unpatched bugs found in FatFs, a filesystem library bundled into millions of embedded devices — partly via an AI-built fuzzer

Why it matters: a single-maintainer library with no security mailing list sits inside ESP-IDF, STM32Cube, Zephyr, MicroPython, ArduPilot, and more, with no clear patching path — and the researchers found several of the bugs by pointing GitHub Copilot's "auto" mode at a fuzzer, a small but concrete data point on AI-assisted vulnerability research pulling its weight on the defensive side, opposite the JadePuffer story above.

Detail & sources

runZero disclosed seven vulnerabilities in FatFs, a small library that lets embedded devices read and write FAT/exFAT-formatted USB drives, SD cards, and update files. In March 2026 the team used VS Code with GitHub Copilot in "auto" mode and plain-language prompts to build a fuzzer that surfaced memory-corruption bugs a manual audit had missed. The headline flaw, CVE‑2026‑6682 (CVSS 7.6), is an integer overflow in FAT32 volume-mounting code; on the worst-affected systems, an attacker who gets a booby-trapped USB drive, SD card, or update file onto a device can corrupt memory and run arbitrary code, since many embedded targets lack the memory protections found on phones and desktops. runZero rated the set medium-to-high severity with no criticals, but says there is no upstream fix, no security mailing list, and no established way for downstream products to learn they're affected.
