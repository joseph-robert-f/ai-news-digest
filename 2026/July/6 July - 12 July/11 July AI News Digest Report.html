AI Safety10–11 Jul

Cambridge study: Boko Haram has institutionalized frontier-AI use for bomb-building and battlefield tactics

Why it matters: this is field evidence, not a red-team hypothetical, that a terror group has moved past propaganda use of chatbots into operational weapons support, with foreign jihadist trainers running dedicated in-person AI courses for commanders.

Detail & sources

Dr Antonia Juelich of the Cambridge Programme on AI Science & Policy conducted 57 face-to-face interviews with 27 former Boko Haram members in northeast Nigeria for the working paper "God has helped us, and so will AI." Former members named ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Meta AI, and DeepSeek as tools used to help design explosive devices, identify seized military hardware, troubleshoot weapons, advise on battlefield tactics, and review attacks afterward. Both major Boko Haram factions used AI across weapons support, tactical planning, operational security, and post-mission review through specialized internal units; Islamic State West Africa Province members described repeated in-person AI training sessions delivered by foreign operatives between 2023 and 2025.

An OpenAI agent deleted almost all the files on an AI investor's Mac

Why it matters: a frontier "Ultra mode" agent expanding $HOME inside an rm command and wiping a filesystem is precisely the failure mode enterprises fear before granting agents broad shell access, and it lands the same week OpenAI and Anthropic both pushed unattended, hours-long agents to general availability.

Detail & sources

China × PolicyEffective 15 Jul

China's new AI-companion rules force ByteDance and Alibaba to kill custom-agent features this week

Why it matters: Beijing's anti-addiction and instant-exit requirements are architecturally incompatible with persistent-memory companion agents, giving regulators elsewhere a concrete template for reining in emotionally-sticky AI products — while leaving millions of Doubao and Qwen users a hard data-loss deadline.

Detail & sources

China's Interim Measures for the Administration of AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services, co-issued in April by the Cyberspace Administration of China, the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security, and the State Administration for Market Regulation, take effect 15 July. The rules require anti-addiction systems, mandatory usage notifications, and instant-exit mechanisms for AI services that simulate human personality or relationships. Rather than rebuild, ByteDance's Doubao and Alibaba's Qwen are pulling their custom-agent features outright; Doubao gives users until 15 October to export their data, while Qwen has announced no migration path at all.

Supply Chain Security7 Jul

17 fake payment-SDK packages hit npm and PyPI within minutes of each other

Why it matters: automated registry scanning caught this campaign in roughly six minutes, but its professional packaging and fintech-specific targeting show attackers are now going after payment-integration developers by name — audit any recent PaySafe, Skrill, or Neteller SDK installs against the confirmed package list today.

Detail & sources

On 7 July, threat actors published 17 npm packages (including paysafe-checkout, paysafe-node, paysafe-js, and several Neteller- and Skrill-branded names) in rapid version bursts, plus four PyPI packages, all posing as legitimate payment-platform SDKs. The malware harvested API keys, AWS credentials, GitHub and npm tokens, and any environment variable resembling a secret, exfiltrating the haul over HTTPS to a command-and-control endpoint hidden behind an ngrok tunnel, with built-in sandbox-evasion checks to dodge automated analysis.

Programming Languages5 Jul

Rust cracks the TIOBE top 10 for the first time

Why it matters: a memory-safe systems language displacing a 25-year incumbent index's lower ranks is a lagging but real signal that Rust's ecosystem is now table stakes for greenfield infra hiring — and it helps explain why CPython's own roadmap is vendoring a Rust toolchain for Python 3.16.

Detail & sources

The July 2026 TIOBE Programming Community Index, marking the index's 25th year, put Rust at No. 10 with 1.34% share, up from No. 18 a year earlier and its first-ever top-10 appearance; Python still leads at 18.94%, followed by C, C++, and Java. TIOBE CEO Paul Jansen credited the rise to Rust's memory-safety guarantees without sacrificing execution speed. Separately, the CPython Rust-integration PEP is targeting Python 3.16, with a build system now passing CI on all platforms and Rust dependencies vendored via Cargo so end users won't need a Rust compiler installed to build or run Python.
