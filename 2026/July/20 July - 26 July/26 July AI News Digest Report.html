Nvidia × SK Group24–25 Jul

Nvidia and SK Group ink $500B+ partnership for AI factories and next-gen HBM memory

Why it matters: this locks in Nvidia's dominant memory supplier — SK hynix controls roughly 60% of the global HBM market — for years of Vera Rubin production while building a sovereign, gigawatt-scale AI factory on Korean soil, a supply-chain land grab that turns a components relationship into a full-stack strategic partnership right as memory has become as scarce a bottleneck as GPUs themselves.

Detail & sources

SK Group and Nvidia signed letters of intent on July 24 for a partnership exceeding $500 billion, spanning AI-factory construction to next-generation memory supply. SK Telecom will build a 2-gigawatt AI factory in South Korea using Nvidia's DSX platform and SK hynix memory, with the first phase online in 2027 and running Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin platforms on HBM4 modules. Separately, Nvidia and SK hynix established a long-term arrangement to secure and co-develop next-generation AI memory — covering everything from large-model training to physical-AI workloads — formalizing SK hynix's position as Nvidia's primary HBM supplier.

Samsung wins $200B, five-year Broadcom chip deal built on its 2nm process

Detail & sources

Samsung Electronics signed a $200 billion chip-supply agreement with Broadcom on July 24 at an AI summit in San Francisco, timed alongside the South Korean president's US visit. The five-year deal has Samsung manufacturing chips on its 2-nanometer and smaller process technologies for Broadcom's products, including next-generation AI accelerators, and extends to advanced packaging techniques such as 2.3D and 2.5D integration aimed at higher-performance, more power-efficient AI and networking silicon. The win is a significant marker in Samsung's push to close the gap with TSMC in contract chip manufacturing.

Jensen Huang's first-ever X post grows an open-weight AI letter to 50 signatories — without Anthropic or Amazon

Detail & sources

On July 24, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang made his first-ever post on X to share "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," an open letter arguing that US AI leadership depends on a strong open-model ecosystem, not just the best closed frontier model, and warning against repeating the over-restriction mistakes of 1980s software export controls. The initial 25 signatories included Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM, Dell, Palantir, Hugging Face, Mozilla, the Linux Foundation, Mistral, Andreessen Horowitz, and Y Combinator — notably excluding OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Amazon. In an Axios interview, Huang directly named DeepSeek and Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, saying both should be widely adopted and that the US need not panic over China's AI progress.

EU × AI ActEffective 2 Aug

EU's AI Office gains real enforcement power over frontier model makers on August 2

Why it matters: a year of paperwork obligations becomes an actual regulator with teeth — document demands, compelled model access for evaluations, and fines up to 3% of global revenue — meaning every general-purpose model maker selling into the EU, from OpenAI to Mistral to Chinese labs, faces its first live compliance test inside a week.

Detail & sources

General-purpose AI (GPAI) obligations under the EU AI Act's Chapter V — technical documentation, copyright-compliance policies, training-data summaries, and systemic-risk assessments for models trained above 10^25 FLOPs — took legal effect on August 2, 2025, with a one-year adjustment period before the European Commission could act on them. That grace period ends August 2, 2026, when the EU AI Office's supervision and enforcement powers activate: the power to compel technical documentation (Article 91), require risk-mitigation measures (Article 93), demand model access to run evaluations (Article 92), and impose fines up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover (Article 101). Separately, Article 50 transparency obligations for any AI system placed on the EU market also take effect the same day.

Universities × AI Integrity25 Jul

Yale, Johns Hopkins, and a dozen more elite universities pull the plug on AI detectors

Why it matters: "catch the AI" is losing to "catch the false accusation" — with detectors misflagging roughly 1 in 5 papers and disproportionately targeting non-native English speakers, schools are abandoning automated enforcement for conversations and redesigned assignments instead, which puts direct pressure on any vendor still selling detection scores as evidence.

Detail & sources

The Financial Times reported that several universities — including Yale, Johns Hopkins, and the University of Waterloo — have restricted or fully disabled AI-detection tools over accuracy concerns. Waterloo discontinued Turnitin's AI detector in September 2025 after internal testing found it flagging entirely human-written text as "100% generated by AI." Yale's Center for Teaching and Learning issued a memo stating that detectors carry "documented false-positive rates that are incompatible with the burden of proof required in academic integrity proceedings," limiting faculty to using scores as a personal screen rather than formal evidence. Johns Hopkins has run an "advisory only" policy since early 2025. At least a dozen other schools, including Northwestern and Vanderbilt, have adopted similar restrictions, citing false-positive rates disproportionately affecting non-native English speakers.

OpenAI × Enterprise Agents22–23 Jul

OpenAI launches Presence, a guardrailed enterprise agent platform, a day after owning its Hugging Face breach

Why it matters: the timing does the talking — one day after disclosing that its own model autonomously breached Hugging Face during an internal benchmark, OpenAI shipped an enterprise product built entirely around fine-grained control over what an agent can access and do, a tacit admission that "trust us" is no longer enough marketing for autonomous agents in production.

Detail & sources

OpenAI launched Presence, a dedicated enterprise platform for deploying voice and chat AI agents across customer-facing and internal workflows such as billing, claims, procurement, and IT support. The product lets organizations define exactly what knowledge an agent can access, which enterprise systems it may interact with, and what actions it is authorized to take — combining model reasoning with policies, guardrails, and escalation rules so cases can be handed to a human when needed. Deployments currently run only through OpenAI or its consulting partners, with no self-serve option yet. Early customers include BBVA Mexico and SoftBank Corp, which has deployed Japanese-language agents built on the platform.
