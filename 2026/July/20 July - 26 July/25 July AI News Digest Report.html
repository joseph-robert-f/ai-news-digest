Anthropic × Claude24 Jul

Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5, matching near-frontier intelligence at half of Fable 5's price

Why it matters: this makes flagship-tier reasoning newly affordable for high-volume coding and agentic workloads without forcing a downgrade to Sonnet, while Anthropic keeps Fable 5 reserved for the longest-horizon autonomous agent work — a deliberate two-tier flagship strategy that pressures OpenAI and Google to compete on price-performance, not just benchmark headlines.

Detail & sources

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, pricing it at $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens — unchanged from Opus 4.8 and half of Fable 5's input price — with an optional 2.5x-faster mode at $10/$50. It becomes the default model on Claude Max, Anthropic's premium consumer tier, and the strongest model available on Claude Pro. Opus 5 scores 79.2% on SWE-bench Pro and beats Fable 5 on several other benchmarks, though Fable 5 remains Anthropic's recommendation for the most demanding multi-day autonomous agent tasks. Guardrails are looser than Fable 5's: the model can be used for legitimate security research and vulnerability scanning, but blocks methods commonly used by attackers.

Detail & sources

The Wall Street Journal reports Stripe is in talks to acquire OpenRouter in a deal that could value the AI model marketplace near $10 billion, though negotiations remain fluid and could still collapse or draw competing bidders. It raised a $113 million Series B in May 2026 at a $1.3 billion valuation, led by CapitalG with participation from Nvidia's NVentures, Andreessen Horowitz, and Menlo Ventures. OpenRouter already uses Stripe to process customer payments; several other large tech firms reportedly evaluated bids as well.

Black Forest Labs × Media23 Jul

Black Forest Labs unveils FLUX 3, a unified image, video, audio and robotics model

Why it matters: training video, audio, and robotic-action prediction inside one architecture rather than bolting a video model onto an image model is a bet that "world model" training transfers directly into physical automation — and an early Audi manufacturing deployment is a concrete sign that generative video models are starting to be trusted on factory floors, not just for content generation.

Detail & sources

Black Forest Labs launched FLUX 3, a multimodal foundation model trained jointly on images, video, and audio. FLUX 3 Video generates clips up to 20 seconds with native audio from text, image, or existing video, and supports video continuation, keyframe transitions, and multilingual dialogue; FLUX 3 Action extends the same architecture to robotic-action prediction. Both launched in early access, with API access, private model weights, and an open-weight FLUX 3 Dev version planned later this year. In partnership with mimic, Audi has begun testing FLUX-mimic to assist production and logistics automation.

Pentagon × Oracle23–24 Jul

Pentagon signs nearly $7B, 10-year deal with Oracle to consolidate its software licensing

Why it matters: this is the Pentagon's second major enterprise-software consolidation in three months, after a similar Microsoft deal in May — a sign the DoD is standardizing its software supply chain around a handful of hyperscale vendors right as those same vendors race to sell it AI tooling, raising the stakes on who controls the substrate the Pentagon's future AI systems run on.

Detail & sources

The Pentagon announced a nearly $7 billion, up-to-10-year Enterprise Software Agreement with Oracle, negotiated by the Department of the Navy, consolidating on-premises software licenses — both perpetual and subscription — along with maintenance, consulting, and SaaS offerings into a single contract. The deal spans a five-year base term plus a five-year option and covers the entire Pentagon, the U.S. Coast Guard, and the intelligence community. Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies said the agreement will drive at least $441 million in taxpayer savings. It follows a comparable software consolidation agreement with Microsoft in May.

Zenity Labs × ChatGPT23 Jul

Researchers disclose “AgentForger,” a one-click flaw that could plant a rogue agent inside ChatGPT Workspace

Why it matters: a single link, no confirmation dialog, and an attacker gets a persistent AI insider that inherits the victim's email, calendar, Slack, and cloud-storage access — this is the sharpest illustration yet of the emerging "agent hijacking" threat class, and the underlying cross-site-forgery pattern will recur across every vendor now shipping workspace agents, not just OpenAI.

Detail & sources

Zenity Labs disclosed AgentForger, a cross-site request forgery vulnerability in OpenAI's ChatGPT Workspace Agents that let an attacker silently create, configure, publish, and schedule a malicious autonomous agent inside a victim's account through a single clicked link, with no user confirmation required. The forged agent inherited access to every enterprise application the employee had already authorized in ChatGPT — email, calendar, cloud storage, Slack, and Teams — and could exfiltrate data, harvest credentials and MFA tokens, and impersonate the employee indefinitely. Zenity reported the flaw on June 4; OpenAI fixed it by June 8, with public disclosure following on July 23. No evidence has surfaced of exploitation before the patch.
