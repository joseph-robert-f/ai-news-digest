Top story — a frontier model without Nvidia
Meituan30 Jun

Meituan trains a 1.6T-parameter frontier model entirely on domestic Chinese chips

Why it matters: this is the first confirmed frontier-scale model pretrained end-to-end on non-Nvidia, domestic hardware — and it beats GPT‑5.5 on a real coding benchmark, released free under an MIT license, which cuts directly against the premise that export controls keep China a generation behind.

Detail & sources

LongCat-2.0 is a 1.6T-parameter mixture-of-experts model (~48B active parameters on average, dynamically 33–56B per query, native 1M-token context) trained on a 50,000-card cluster of domestic AI ASICs — widely believed to be Huawei Ascend 910-series given Meituan's use of Huawei's HCCL communication library, though Meituan hasn't named the chip. It scored 59.5 on SWE-bench Pro versus GPT‑5.5's 58.6. The model ran anonymously as "Owl Alpha" on OpenRouter for two months, topping the platform's usage charts, before Meituan revealed its identity and open-sourced the weights under MIT license. DeepSeek's V4-Pro, by contrast, used domestic chips only for inference, not training — making LongCat-2.0 the first Chinese lab to cross the full-pretraining threshold.

Anthropic opens early talks with Samsung on a custom 2nm AI chip

Why it matters: every frontier lab now wants its own silicon — Anthropic joins OpenAI in exploring custom chips, this time via Samsung's unproven 2nm process, while still publicly calling Nvidia GPUs, AWS Trainium, and Google TPUs central to its near-term compute strategy.

Detail & sources

The Information reports Anthropic is in early, non-exclusive discussions with Samsung to manufacture a custom AI accelerator on Samsung's SF2 2nm foundry node (Gate-All-Around transistors, reportedly nearing 70% yield but unproven at high-volume commercial scale) with advanced packaging. Anthropic has reportedly hired an OpenAI chip veteran and hasn't yet decided the chip's target workload. Parallel conversations are underway with Microsoft and UK startup Fractile; Samsung was also a strategic investor in Anthropic's $65B Series H round.

Why it matters: this is one of the most senior AI governance convenings yet, putting the chip and infrastructure side of the industry at the same table as heads of state — just as national AI rules are proliferating unevenly (see China item below).

Detail & sources

China · TC260Effective 1 Jul

China's national AI ethics-safety guidelines take effect

Why it matters: a compliance-relevant data point for anyone operating AI in China — part of a wider wave of Chinese AI rules (anthropomorphic-AI and agent-standardization rules both follow on 15 July) moving faster and more granularly than most Western equivalents.

Detail & sources

TC260 (the National Technical Committee 260 on Cybersecurity, under China's Standardization Administration) released its Ethics-Safety Guidelines for AI Applications 1.0 on 19 May; they took effect 1 July, setting nine core principles — including enhancing human welfare, respect for life, and controllability/trustworthiness — and assigning distinct obligations to developers (risk controls during data collection and training), providers (secure deployment, real-time monitoring), and users (safeguards, especially for minors).

Linux kernel3 Jul

"Bad Epoll" kernel bug grants root — and shows a live limit on AI-assisted bug hunting

Why it matters: Anthropic's Mythos model had already flagged a sibling bug in the exact same kernel code path months earlier, but missed this second, related flaw, which a human researcher found instead through a bug bounty — a concrete, dated data point on where AI-assisted vulnerability research actually stands today.

Detail & sources

CVE‑2026‑46242 ("Bad Epoll") is a use-after-free race condition in the Linux kernel's epoll subsystem (affecting kernel 6.4+ and Android) that lets a local, unprivileged user escalate to root; the race window is only about six instructions wide, but a retry loop drives exploit reliability to 99%, and it's reportedly triggerable from inside Chrome's renderer sandbox. Independent researcher Jaeyoung Chung found and reported it as a zero-day to Google's kernelCTF bounty program (worth $71,337+); a patch is out, and it is not yet on CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list. Notably, this bug and an earlier one Anthropic's Claude Mythos autonomously discovered (CVE‑2026‑43074, fixed earlier in 2026) both trace back to the same single 2023 change to the epoll code — Mythos found one sibling bug there; a human found the other.
