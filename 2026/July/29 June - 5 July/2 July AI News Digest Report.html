UpdateAdded 2 Jul, midday ET

Meta's leaked compute-resale pivot, not Korea, is the real trigger — and it's spreading to neoclouds

Why it matters: the causal story behind this morning's chip selloff changed by midday — it isn't primarily Seoul's spending plan, it's Meta signalling it may have more AI compute than it needs. That flips the industry's core assumption that GPU demand always outstrips supply, and the stocks most exposed to Meta's own contracts got hit hardest.

Detail & sources

Bloomberg reported 1 July that Meta is drawing up plans for "Meta Compute," a cloud business — led by infrastructure chief Santosh Janardhan, Daniel Gross, and president Dina Powell McCormick — to resell excess AI capacity, either as managed model access (Bedrock-style) or raw GPU capacity (CoreWeave-style). Zuckerberg said at May's shareholder meeting that outside firms were approaching Meta "almost every week" for spare compute; Muse Spark's underperformance versus Claude and GPT‑5.5 left internal utilization below plan. Meta shares jumped 8–11% on the news. By Wednesday's close the read-through hit the rest of the sector: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.3% (KLA −12%, Lam Research −9.7%, Applied Materials −10%, Micron/Sandisk −10.6%), and neoclouds whose backlogs depend on Meta contracts were hit hardest — CoreWeave (which has roughly $21B in Meta-linked commitments) closed −14%, and Nebius (on the hook for $12–27B of Meta capacity through 2027+) closed −17%, roughly $12B of market cap in a day. Samsung and SK Hynix, −7%/−9% at this morning's Kospi open per the item above, closed the session down 9.06%/14.57% — nearly double the opening move.

Separately, Axios reports OpenAI now expects GPT‑5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna to clear the trusted-partner gate and reach general availability by mid-July, roughly two weeks after the 26 June limited preview — the first concrete date range since the 1–2 July item above.
