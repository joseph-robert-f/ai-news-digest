Anthropic × PentagonUnsealed 2 Jul

Court emails: Pentagon told Amodei a deal was "very close" a day after blacklisting Anthropic

Why it matters: the documents show the fight was never really about access to Claude — it was about whether an AI lab can hold a hard line against autonomous weapons and domestic surveillance against its largest possible customer, and the paper trail undercuts the government's own justification for the blacklist.

Detail & sources

Emails unsealed 2 July in Anthropic's lawsuit against the Pentagon (N.D. Cal.) show CEO Dario Amodei held two consistent, explicit dealbreakers across months of negotiation with Emil Michael, the Pentagon's Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering: Claude could not be used to operate fully autonomous weapons (no human in the loop at engagement) or for domestic mass surveillance. Talks soured in January; Michael later wrote the guardrails were "just not workable" and, according to the filings, that Anthropic's proposed contract language would "completely remove our redlines." The day before Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth publicly designated Anthropic a supply-chain security risk, Michael emailed Amodei that the two sides were "very close" on terms — before Anthropic had been told about the coming designation. The presiding judge, Lin, quoted that exchange directly and called it "exceedingly difficult to square" with the government's contemporaneous framing of Anthropic as a hostile actor. Anthropic separately lost an appellate bid in April to block enforcement of the blacklist while the underlying suit proceeds.

Anthropic × China3 Jul

Anthropic moves to shut Singapore and VPN workarounds Chinese firms used to reach Claude

Why it matters: the same week Anthropic is fighting the Pentagon over military-use guardrails, it's tightening enforcement of its own China ban — closing gaps that let Ant Financial and ByteDance staff route around it via offshore entities and personal VPNs.

Detail & sources

Per the Financial Times, Ant Financial gave employees corporate Claude accounts tied to a Singapore-incorporated entity, while ByteDance reimbursed staff for personal Claude subscriptions accessed over VPNs; other firms reached Claude through foreign-incorporated units running on Azure. Anthropic's terms have barred access for any company more than 50%-owned by China-, Russia-, Iran-, or North Korea-based entities since 2025 — CEO Dario Amodei said in February the policy has cost "several hundred million dollars" in forgone revenue. Enforcement now includes monitoring account time zones and usage patterns for signs of relay/transfer-station behavior, plus ID-and-selfie verification for flagged accounts (added April). None of this breaks US or Chinese law; it enforces Anthropic's own terms of service.

Security & software engineering
JetBrains2 Jul

JetBrains patches critical Hub identity-service bugs — auth bypass to admin, predictable account recovery

Why it matters: Hub is the single sign-on backbone behind YouTrack and TeamCity — three chained, critical-severity bugs here mean an unauthenticated attacker could potentially seize admin accounts across an org's entire JetBrains server stack, not just one product.

Detail & sources

CVE‑2026‑50242 (CWE‑306) lets an attacker bypass authentication via direct database access to reach administrative functions. CVE‑2026‑56141 (CVSS 9.8, CWE‑338) stems from predictable account-restore codes generated by a weak RNG, letting an attacker enumerate valid codes and hijack any account, including admins. CVE‑2026‑56142 (CWE‑915) allows an authenticated user to attach unauthorized authentication details to an existing account for privilege escalation. All three are fixed in Hub 2026.1.13757, with backports for the 2025.x and 2024.x branches; JetBrains advises prioritizing internet-facing Hub instances, rotating credentials, and auditing recent account-recovery and auth-mapping activity.
