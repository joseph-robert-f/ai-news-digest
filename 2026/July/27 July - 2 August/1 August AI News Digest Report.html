Apple closes the Big Tech earnings season with a record quarter and a 6% stock drop; combined hyperscaler AI capex nears $725B

Detail & sources

Apple reported fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $109.42 billion on July 30, up 16% year over year and its best June quarter on record, beating a $108.79 billion estimate; EPS of $2.02 topped the $1.88 consensus and net income came in near $29.8 billion. Legal & copyright
GEMA × Suno31 Jul

Munich court hands Suno its first EU defeat: training on copyrighted music without a license infringes, full stop

Why it matters: this is the first European ruling to say plainly that generative AI training itself — not just what a model later outputs — requires a license, and it lands while the parallel Sony Music case in the US is headed toward its own summary-judgment hearing on the identical legal question.

Detail & sources

The Munich Regional Court's 42nd Civil Chamber, under Judge Elke Schwager, ruled on July 31 that Suno infringed copyrights held by GEMA, Germany's music-rights collecting society, by training its generative models on GEMA-represented works without a license. The court found in GEMA's favor on nearly every point: reproducing copyrighted recordings during training violates reproduction rights, and making AI-generated outputs available to users separately violates the right of public communication. Suno must now disclose revenue tied to the infringing use so damages can be calculated, and the company says it is evaluating its options, including an appeal. The ruling is the first in Europe to establish that generative AI companies must license the catalogs they learn from, a question still unresolved in the US, where a summary-judgment hearing in Sony Music's suit against Suno is before Chief Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV.

Policy & governance
EO 14409deadline 1 Aug

Trump's frontier-AI executive order hits its first deadline: a classified, secret-criteria benchmarking regime

Why it matters: the framework is officially "voluntary," but the practical effect — the government deciding in secret which models count as a security risk, with up to 30 days of pre-release access before anyone outside a small circle sees the criteria — is the most direct federal insertion into frontier-model release timelines to date.

Detail & sources

Executive Order 14409, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," set a 60-day deadline — landing August 1, 2026 — for the Departments of Treasury, War (acting through the NSA), and Homeland Security (acting through CISA) to design a frontier-model review framework. Section 3 of the order requires those agencies to build and maintain a classified benchmarking process that assesses advanced cyber capabilities in AI models and designates which ones qualify as "covered frontier models"; the NSA director makes the designation calls, and the specific criteria and thresholds are being kept classified, meaning developers won't know in advance what triggers coverage. Designated models are subject to up to 30 days of pre-release federal access, with agencies and developers jointly selecting trusted partners for that early review. Legal analysts have noted the framework is voluntary on paper but functions as mandatory in practice, since developers have little practical ability to opt out once a model is designated.

DeepSeek31 Jul

DeepSeek's retrained V4-Flash-0731 beats its own pricier flagship on every published agent benchmark

Why it matters: this is a post-training-only retrain of an existing model, not a new architecture, and it now outperforms DeepSeek's own flagship on agentic and coding tasks at roughly a thirtieth of frontier Western pricing — a reminder that the price-performance gap between open and closed models keeps narrowing on the cheap side of the ledger, not just the capability side.

Detail & sources

DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 into public beta on July 31, taking the same architecture and parameter count as its April Flash preview — 284 billion total parameters, 13 billion active per token via mixture-of-experts, 1 million token context — and re-running post-training with a substantially strengthened pipeline focused on agentic workflows and reasoning. The retrained model scores higher than DeepSeek's own V4-Pro-Preview on all nine agent and coding benchmarks the company published; on Terminal-Bench 2.1 specifically, the score jumped from 61.8 in the preview build to 82.7. Pricing is $0.14 per million input tokens and $0.27 per million output tokens, dramatically undercutting frontier Western model pricing even after this week's OpenAI cuts.

Apollo × Wages30 Jul

Apollo study: AI isn't cutting jobs, it's cutting pay — and almost all of the damage lands on the lowest earners

Why it matters: this is a reversal from the same Apollo economist who previously downplayed AI's labor-market impact, and by using actual AI-usage data instead of theoretical exposure scores, it makes the more specific and more testable claim that firms are pocketing AI's productivity gains as suppressed wages rather than headcount cuts — concentrated almost entirely on service workers and the bottom wage quartile.

Detail & sources

Apollo Global Management economists Sania Edlich and Torsten Slok published a study on July 30 matching real AI-usage data from the Anthropic Economic Index against Bureau of Labor Statistics wage and employment figures across 321 matched occupations from 2015 to 2025. High-AI-exposure occupations saw real wage growth run 6.7 percentage points slower than comparable lower-exposure occupations since 2023, with no detectable increase in job losses. The effect is heavily concentrated at the bottom of the income scale: service workers saw a 24.3% decline and the bottom wage quartile a 10.7% decline, while top earners showed no significant effect. The authors estimate affected workers are collectively losing about $28 billion a year in wages that would otherwise have accrued. The finding marks a shift from Slok's earlier 2026 commentary, which had argued AI's employment effects were minimal.

OpenAI × Pricing30 Jul

OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Luna price 80% and Terra 20%, three weeks after launch

Why it matters: a five-figure percentage cut on a model family that launched barely three weeks ago is an unusually fast concession on price, and it lands the same week DeepSeek's cheap retrained model is climbing the agent-benchmark leaderboards — the cost side of the model race is moving as fast as the capability side.

Detail & sources

OpenAI announced on July 30 that it is cutting GPT-5.6 Luna's price by 80%, to 20 cents per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens (from $1 and $6), and GPT-5.6 Terra's by 20%, to $2 per million input and $12 per million output (from $2.50 and $15); pricing for the flagship Sol model is unchanged, though OpenAI added a faster Sol option in the API. OpenAI attributed the cuts to efficiency gains uncovered during GPT-5.6's development, including the model's own ability to optimize production code and improve token generation, and said the lower prices are reflected directly in usage accounting inside Codex and ChatGPT Work.
