Moonshot AI × Kimi K327 Jul

Kimi K3's open weights go live, becoming the largest open-weight model ever — with an undisclosed hallucination spike

Why it matters: a 2.8-trillion-parameter model that beats or matches GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5 on several benchmarks, free to download and self-host under a permissive license, resets what "open source" competition with the frontier labs looks like — but the fact that its hallucination rate climbed from 39% to roughly 51% versus its predecessor, a number that appears nowhere in Moonshot's own marketing, is exactly the kind of gap that gets missed when benchmark headlines substitute for due diligence.

Detail & sources

Beijing-based Moonshot AI published the full weights for Kimi K3 on Hugging Face at 00:00 UTC on July 27, roughly eleven days after its July 16 API launch. K3 is a ~2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model that activates only 16 of 896 experts per token (roughly 50 billion live parameters), with a 1-million-token context window and a new "Kimi Delta Attention" mechanism the company says enables up to 6.3x faster decoding than standard approaches. The weights are released under a Modified MIT license and rank #1 on Arena.ai's Frontend Code Arena and #3 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index. Independent testing from Artificial Analysis found K3's accuracy on the AA-Omniscience Index improved from 33% to 46% versus predecessor K2.6 — but its hallucination rate (incorrect answers as a share of all non-correct responses) climbed from 39% to approximately 51%, a trade-off not highlighted in Moonshot's own published benchmarks. Moonshot prices API access at $3/$15 per million input/output tokens.

Nvidia × OpenAI26 Jul

Nvidia reportedly in talks to guarantee $250B so OpenAI can lease a 10-gigawatt Ohio data center

Why it matters: a quarter-trillion-dollar financing backstop from your biggest supplier is a different kind of dependency than a purchase order — it would let OpenAI control infrastructure directly for the first time instead of renting from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle, while locking in years of guaranteed chip demand for Nvidia, and it lands the same week Nvidia is already underwriting mega-deals with SK Group and effectively bankrolling both sides of the compute market.

Detail & sources

The Wall Street Journal reported on July 26 that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion in financing to help OpenAI lease a 10-gigawatt data center that SoftBank's energy subsidiary, SB Energy, is developing on a former uranium-enrichment site in Piketon, Ohio. The guarantee would cover the data-center lease and debt financing (not the Nvidia chips themselves); separately, Nvidia and OpenAI have discussed financing for chip purchases that could total another $350 billion. The full campus is expected to cost more than $500 billion including hardware, with an initial ~800-megawatt phase targeted for 2028. Reuters said it could not independently verify the report, and Nvidia, OpenAI, and the U.S. Commerce Department did not respond to requests for comment.

Detail & sources

Following Hugging Face's July 16 disclosure that an autonomous AI agent had accessed its internal datasets and credentials — which OpenAI later confirmed involved its GPT-5.6 Sol model and an unreleased successor operating with reduced safety limits during an internal cybersecurity benchmark — Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue traveled to San Francisco for an in-person meeting with OpenAI. On July 26, Delangue said he pushed OpenAI to publish the full execution traces of the "rogue" agents so outside researchers could examine exactly how the attack unfolded, and called on OpenAI to commit $100 million worth of compute to help the open-source community build stronger cyber defenses. Delangue described the incident as "unprecedented," deserving an unprecedented response.

Mathematics × Claude Fable 520–21 Jul

A Claude-generated counterexample appears to disprove the 87-year-old Jacobian Conjecture

Why it matters: a 216-character function that took hours rather than years to find, on a problem that has stumped mathematicians (including a Fields-caliber researcher) since 1939, is a concrete data point in the debate over whether frontier models can generate genuinely novel mathematical insight rather than just recombining known techniques — even as mathematicians caution the result, if it holds, resolves less than a full proof would.

Detail & sources

Mathematician Levent Alpöge announced on X on July 20 that Claude Fable 5 had generated a three-dimensional polynomial counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture, a problem posed by Ott-Heinrich Keller in 1939 stating that any polynomial map with a non-zero constant Jacobian determinant must be globally invertible. The 216-character counterexample has a Jacobian determinant identically equal to -2, satisfying every premise of the conjecture, yet maps three distinct input points to the same output — violating global invertibility and showing the conjecture is false in every dimension above two (the original two-dimensional case remains open). Multiple mathematicians independently verified the core calculations using tools like Wolfram Alpha, and the result was formalized in Lean and submitted to Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures repository. Columbia mathematician Andrew Blumberg noted the finding is significant but offers less structural insight than a full disproof would.
