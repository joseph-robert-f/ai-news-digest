Markets × AI Capex28 Jul

Kospi suffers its worst session since March as chip stocks crater on AI-financing and China fears

Why it matters: when memory-chip stocks that make up more than half a benchmark index shed 13–15% in a single session, and the flight to safety accidentally crowns Apple — a company that mostly rents its AI compute rather than building it — the world's most valuable public company for the first time in a year, that's the market casting a verdict on the entire "buy chips first, ask about ROI later" era of AI capex, not just a bad day for Samsung.

Detail & sources

South Korea's Kospi index plunged 10.8% on July 28 to close at 6,023.66, its steepest drop since a record 12.06% crash on March 4, 2026, and its fourth-largest single-day decline on record; trading was briefly halted by a circuit breaker. Samsung Electronics fell 13.4% and SK Hynix tumbled 14.7% — the two chipmakers together account for more than half of the Kospi's weighting. Japan's Nikkei 225 and Taiwan's benchmark each fell nearly 4%, and US futures pointed lower before the bell. The rout traces to Sunday's Wall Street Journal report that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee roughly $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's Ohio data-center lease, which reignited investor worry about "circular financing" — chipmakers effectively bankrolling their own customers' demand — compounded by rising competition from Chinese chipmakers and model labs. Nvidia shares fell as much as 5% on Monday, a slide that, combined with a 22%-plus year-to-date rally, let Apple's stock close at a record high and reclaim the title of world's most valuable public company (roughly $4.94 trillion market cap versus Nvidia's $4.75 trillion) for the first time since Nvidia took the crown in June 2025, with investors rewarding Apple specifically for renting AI compute instead of building it.

Policy × Export Controls22–27 Jul

The White House's Kimi K3 "distillation" accusation against Moonshot stays unresolved, with sanctions still on the table

Why it matters: a senior US official publicly accusing a Chinese lab of running a covert operation to strip-mine Anthropic's models and illegally acquire embargoed Nvidia chips — with the Treasury Secretary floating sanctions — is a different order of confrontation than the usual open-model benchmark wars, and it's playing out in the same week Moonshot published Kimi K3's full weights for anyone to inspect.

Detail & sources

White House Office of Science and Technology Policy Director Michael Kratsios said on July 22 that the US has evidence Moonshot AI "distilled Anthropic's Fable" to build Kimi K3, alleging the company built "a sophisticated internal platform designed to perform large-scale distillation against U.S. models" while cycling through access methods to avoid detection, and that Moonshot obtained export-controlled Nvidia GB300 servers, including access to GB300s located in Thailand. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said covert, industrial-scale distillation that crosses into IP theft could trigger sanctions or Entity List additions. As of this week, no sanctions or Entity List designation had been imposed, and independent researchers — including Braden Hancock of the Laude Institute — have publicly questioned the timeline, noting Fable 5 was only re-released July 1 after briefly being pulled for export-control reasons, leaving a narrow window for the alleged distillation. Anthropic has not presented evidence tying Kimi K3 specifically to distillation, though it made a similar accusation against Moonshot in February.

Microsoft × Cybersecurity27 Jul

Microsoft ships its first in-house cybersecurity model, undercutting Anthropic and OpenAI on price

Why it matters: Microsoft choosing to build and ship a specialized security model rather than just keep routing traffic to OpenAI signals that burning frontier-model tokens on every vulnerability scan doesn't work at enterprise scale — the next layer of competition is who can do a specific job well enough for a fraction of the price.

Detail & sources

Microsoft AI introduced MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, a 137-billion-parameter (5B active) sparse mixture-of-experts model fine-tuned from MAI-Code-1-Flash with a 256K context window, built into Microsoft's MDASH multi-agent security platform. Combined with GPT-5.4 handling escalations, the MDASH/MAI-Cyber-1-Flash pairing scored 95.95% on CyberGym, a benchmark testing whether AI systems can find real vulnerabilities by reasoning over large codebases — ahead of Anthropic's Mythos 5 (83.8%) and OpenAI's own GPT-5.6 Sol (83.6%) and GPT-5.5 Cyber (85.6%). Microsoft says MAI-Cyber-1-Flash handles roughly 90% of tasks itself, only escalating the hardest cases to GPT-5.4, which it says cuts overall security-agent costs by about 50%.

Cognizant becomes a "Global Premier Partner" for Claude, citing 40% faster contract reviews in live deployments

Agility Robotics agrees to go public in a $2.5B SPAC deal, aiming to be the first public humanoid-robot company

Why it matters: more than $300 million in booked, multi-year robots-as-a-service revenue from roughly 1,000 deployed units is a real, if early, commercial base for humanoid robotics — going public via SPAC gets Agility capital and a ticker fast, but it also puts a still-nascent business under public-market scrutiny sooner than a private raise would have.

Detail & sources

Agility Robotics, maker of the Digit humanoid robot, announced a definitive business combination agreement with Churchill Capital Corp XI on July 28, valuing the combined company at roughly $2.5 billion and set to trade under the ticker AGLT once the deal closes, subject to shareholder and SEC approval. The transaction includes about $420 million held in Churchill's SPAC trust plus a roughly $200 million PIPE round led by existing investor Foxconn — more than $600 million in gross proceeds combined. Agility says it has deployed close to 100 Digit robots commercially, mostly in logistics and manufacturing, underpinning more than $300 million in booked multi-year revenue under a robots-as-a-service model.

AMD × Cerebras23 Jul

AMD and Cerebras split AI inference between GPUs and wafer-scale chips for up to 5x efficiency

Why it matters: instead of competing head-on with Nvidia's all-in-one GPU racks, AMD and Cerebras are betting that splitting inference into two specialized stages beats a single vendor doing both — a disaggregated architecture that, if it works, gives enterprises a lower-cost, lower-latency alternative for high-throughput agentic workloads.

Detail & sources

AMD and Cerebras announced a joint inference solution at AMD's Advancing AI 2026 event on July 23, combining AMD Helios rack-scale systems with Cerebras' Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE) in a single disaggregated pipeline: Helios processes prompts and large context windows, while the WSE handles token generation for latency-sensitive applications. The companies say the pairing can deliver up to 5x higher tokens-per-second-per-watt versus prior Cerebras-only configurations. Cerebras plans to deploy AMD Helios systems in its own data centers, with the joint solution first available through Cerebras Cloud in the second half of 2026.
