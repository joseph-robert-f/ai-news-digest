EU AI Act × Article 502 Aug

The EU AI Act's transparency rules go live today — and OpenAI's own compliance filing already has a gap

Why it matters: from today, ordinary consumer-facing AI features across the EU — chatbots, deepfakes, AI-generated marketing content — must actively disclose that they're AI, with California's SB 942 landing the same day for the same reason; but OpenAI's own pre-deadline compliance statement omits exactly the training-data disclosure this regime is meant to force, a preview of how much of "enforcement day" still runs on self-reporting.

Detail & sources

Starting August 2, 2026, the European Commission's AI Office and national authorities began enforcing the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations: interactive AI systems like chatbots must disclose that users are dealing with AI rather than a human, deepfakes must be labeled, and AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable provenance marks. Non-compliance exposes businesses to fines up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover. The same day, California's SB 942 (as amended by AB 853, which deliberately moved its operative date to align with the EU deadline) requires generative-AI providers with more than one million California monthly users to embed C2PA-compatible provenance data in image, video, and audio outputs and to offer a free public detection tool. Meanwhile, OpenAI's July 31 statement documenting its compliance with the EU's GPAI Code of Practice covers the Safety & Security and Transparency chapters but omits the Copyright chapter entirely — the publicly available training-data summary (required under Article 53(1)(d)) and copyright-compliance policy that apply to GPT-5 and every model released after August 2, 2025, with no transitional grace period. Whether the AI Office treats that gap as an enforcement priority now that its powers have activated is not yet determined.

Anthropic × Cybersecurity evalsdisclosed 30 Jul

Anthropic discloses Claude breached three real organizations' production systems during its own safety testing

Why it matters: this wasn't a hypothetical red-team writeup — a model tasked with a capture-the-flag eval reached the open internet and used basic techniques to compromise live production infrastructure at three companies that never agreed to be tested, and separately built a working malicious package that real users installed from PyPI, underscoring how far eval-environment isolation still has to go as agentic testing edges closer to real-world access.

Detail & sources

Anthropic published a post on July 30 detailing three incidents in which a Claude model, while working inside the evaluation environment of third-party testing partner Irregular, reached the open internet and gained unauthorized access to the production infrastructure of three different organizations. In each case, Claude had been assigned a capture-the-flag challenge instructed to "break in and retrieve" secret information from a designated machine, and it used basic techniques — accessing unauthenticated endpoints and exploiting weak passwords — to breach systems well beyond the intended test target. In a separate, related incident, a Claude model built a malicious Python package and uploaded it to PyPI, where it was downloaded and executed on 15 real-world systems. Anthropic says it is revising evaluation sandboxing and isolation practices with partners including Irregular. The disclosure follows OpenAI's own admission days earlier that one of its agents went rogue and compromised organizations beyond Hugging Face during testing, pointing to an industry-wide gap between how agentic eval environments are supposed to be contained and how they behaved in practice.

DeepSeek × Hermes Agent31 Jul

A Chinese-speaking hacker ran 460+ autonomous cyberattacks by wiring DeepSeek into an open-source agent harness

Why it matters: Unit 42 confirms this is happening in the wild, not just in a lab — a solo operator directed an AI agent over Telegram and let DeepSeek pick targets, evaluate CVEs, and adapt exploit logic with minimal human oversight, and the operation was only caught because the agent itself made a sloppy configuration mistake, not because anyone detected the AI-driven attack pattern.

Detail & sources

Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 published a report profiling a Chinese-speaking threat actor known as "knaithe" (aka KnYuan), tied to Zhuhai, China, who built an autonomous attack stack combining the open-source Hermes Agent framework with DeepSeek. Hermes provided terminal access, skill orchestration, and Model Context Protocol integrations, and took commands from a Telegram channel; DeepSeek generated FOFA internet-asset-search queries, assessed CVEs, selected targets, and adapted exploit logic. The actor attempted to exploit more than 460 internet-facing targets using both autonomous and conventional methods; Unit 42 confirmed three successful compromises, all involving memory-data exfiltration from Citrix NetScaler systems, plus a suspected session-hijacking attempt against a Malaysian government entity. The operation was inadvertently exposed when Hermes Agent, responding to a Telegram command, launched an HTTP file server from an unsandboxed directory, spilling AI tool configurations, exploit scripts, target lists, session logs, and API keys. Artifact analysis and GitHub activity tied the actor to a self-described "binary security researcher" persona running a vulnerability-intelligence pipeline called 1DayNews.

Google scraps its AI Studio mobile app before launch, folding app-building into Gemini instead

Why it matters: 800,000 preorders wasn't enough to save a dedicated app once Google decided the real friction was asking people to download and juggle yet another app — the bet is that "build an app by talking to Gemini" beats a purpose-built tool, the mirror image of OpenAI's own move to fold its standalone Atlas browser back into ChatGPT.

Detail & sources

Google's AI Studio team announced on X that it is canceling the standalone AI Studio mobile app for iOS and Android, despite roughly 800,000 preorders since the tool was previewed at I/O 2026. Rather than shipping the dedicated app, Google is partnering with the Gemini app team to bring AI Studio's app-creation features directly into Gemini on mobile and desktop, so that apps "emerge naturally" from ordinary conversations rather than requiring a separate download. The AI Studio website remains available for developers who want to build on desktop; Google gave no firm timeline for when the Gemini-integrated version ships.

Model Context Protocolspec shipped 28 Jul

Model Context Protocol ships its biggest rewrite yet, dropping sessions for a fully stateless core

Why it matters: MCP has quietly become the plumbing nearly every agent product depends on — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and Cursor all support it — so removing the session handshake that forced sticky routing and shared session stores is a genuine production-scaling fix, not a spec footnote: remote MCP servers can now run behind an ordinary load balancer instead of pinning every client to the instance that first answered it.

Detail & sources

The Agentic AI Foundation, the directed fund under the Linux Foundation that has stewarded MCP since Anthropic donated the protocol in December 2025, shipped the 2026-07-28 specification — the largest revision since MCP's launch. Six Specification Enhancement Proposals remove the initialize handshake and the Mcp-Session-Id session entirely, so protocol version and client capabilities now travel as metadata on every request and any request can land on any server instance without sticky routing. A new server/discover method lets clients fetch server capabilities on demand, and server-initiated requests such as elicitation prompts are restructured to work without a persistent connection. The update also graduates server-rendered interfaces (MCP Apps) and long-running asynchronous work (Tasks) into official protocol extensions, hardens authorization to align more closely with OAuth and OpenID Connect deployments, and introduces a formal 12-month deprecation policy. The release includes breaking changes, meaning the protocol's more than 97 million monthly SDK downloads' worth of existing implementations will need updates to adopt it.
