Amazon crosses $200B in quarterly revenue for the first time; net income triples on its Anthropic stake

Why it matters: this is the strongest of the week's three hyperscaler earnings reports — AWS's growth rate is accelerating, not just its revenue base, and Amazon's own AI and chips businesses are now scaling at triple-digit rates, giving the clearest signal yet that this earnings season is rewarding AI capex rather than punishing it.

Detail & sources

Amazon reported Q2 2026 revenue of $200.6 billion, its first quarter above $200 billion, versus a roughly $196.4 billion estimate. EPS of $5.75 came in more than triple consensus (about $1.82 expected), and net income reached $62.6 billion, up from $18.2 billion a year earlier — a jump driven largely by $53.4 billion in non-operating pre-tax gains tied to Amazon's investment in Anthropic, formalized through the companies' 10-year, $100 billion deal to run Claude on AWS Trainium chips. CEO Andy Jassy said Amazon's AI and chips businesses each now exceed $25 billion in annualized revenue, both growing at triple-digit percentage rates. Amazon raised its 2026 capex guidance from $200 billion to $220 billion, citing higher memory costs; shares rose about 9.5% in after-hours trading.

Cybersecurity & AI agents
Ruflo × RufRoot29 Jul

Maximum-severity flaw in a popular open-source agent harness let anyone on the network take over the container and poison agent memory

Why it matters: this is a concrete illustration of the exact class of risk the industry has spent the past two weeks debating in the abstract — a self-hosted agent framework shipped with 233 tools, including arbitrary shell execution, exposed to the network with zero authentication by default, no exotic exploit required.

Detail & sources

Security firm Noma Security disclosed CVE-2026-59726 ("RufRoot"), a maximum CVSS-10.0 flaw in Ruflo, an open-source agent meta-harness used to orchestrate Anthropic Claude Code and OpenAI Codex agents. Ruflo's default docker-compose deployment ran its MCP Bridge — an Express.js server exposing 233 tools, including a terminal_execute tool for arbitrary shell commands — over HTTP with no authentication, meaning a single crafted request could grant an attacker shell access inside the container, expose stored provider API keys, and poison entries in Ruflo's AgentDB long-term memory store to manipulate future agent behavior. Noma disclosed the flaw responsibly on June 30, 2026, and the maintainer shipped a fix in version 3.16.3 within 24 hours; all versions before that release remain vulnerable if not upgraded.

PwC × GPTZero29 Jul

PwC becomes the third Big Four firm this year caught publishing AI-hallucinated research

Why it matters: this keeps happening at the exact firms enterprises pay to be the adult in the room on AI adoption — a fabricated framework and a nonexistent academic study aren't formatting slip-ups, they're a signal that AI-drafted deliverables are shipping to clients without anyone actually checking the citations.

Detail & sources

AI-detection firm GPTZero published an investigation into four "thought leadership" reports issued by PwC Middle East between 2024 and 2026, covering agentic AI adoption playbooks, government digital-services guidance, and electric- and autonomous-vehicle market forecasts. The reports contained citations linking to web pages that don't contain the claimed evidence or don't exist at all, an academic paper on Riyadh air quality that appears to have been invented outright, and a proprietary "Citizen Pulse" framework that GPTZero's researchers could find no public evidence of outside the report itself — PwC has since removed that report from its website. One footnote's source URL simply ended in "chatgpt.com." The finding follows GPTZero investigations earlier this year that led EY and KPMG to retract their own AI-hallucinated reports.

EU × EuroHPC30 Jul

Brussels opens bidding for seven AI "Gigafactories" backed by €30B, aiming to close Europe's compute gap

Why it matters: Europe has spent the past two years mostly regulating AI; this is its clearest bid yet to actually build the compute capacity to compete on it, and the structure — public money plus required private capital, run by industry consortia rather than the Commission itself — is a bet that public funding alone won't close the gap with the US and China.

Detail & sources

The EuroHPC Joint Undertaking launched a formal call for tenders on July 30 inviting consortia to build and operate up to seven AI Gigafactories across the EU, backed by €10 billion in public funding and an expected €20 billion in matched private investment, for more than €30 billion in total planned investment. Four smaller facilities, each requiring at least 75,000 AI chips, are eligible for up to €500 million in public funding apiece. Applications may come from consortia or special-purpose vehicles combining companies, public bodies, and investors; the submission deadline is November 12, 2026, with winning projects selected in early 2027 and operations expected to begin within 18 months of selection. The push is explicitly framed as a tech-sovereignty initiative to reduce Europe's reliance on US and Asian AI infrastructure.
