Detail & sources

The Keelung District Prosecutors Office detained an Nvidia employee, identified only by the surname Chang, on July 28 as part of an expanding investigation into the alleged smuggling of AI servers containing restricted Nvidia chips to China. Investigators had searched Chang's home and workplace on July 24; prosecutors allege the defendants falsified paperwork to facilitate the export of roughly 50 Super Micro servers, some of which passed through Taiwan customs and continued on through Japan before reaching China. Seven people are now being held in the probe, including two Super Micro employees and one from Taiwan-listed Albatron Technology. The case follows the US Justice Department's arrest of a Super Micro co-founder two months earlier in a related case alleging billions of dollars of Nvidia AI chips were smuggled to China.

AI research & agents
Benchmark28 Jul

New benchmark: even the best AI agent fails 64% of tasks that just require following a written policy

Why it matters: most agent benchmarks measure whether a task gets done; this one measures whether the agent stays inside the rules while doing it — and the result suggests that handing agents a company handbook and expecting them to comply, unsupervised, over a long task is not yet close to solved, which matters directly for any enterprise deploying agents against real operating procedures.

Detail & sources

Researchers published HANDBOOK.md, a benchmark of 65 agentic tasks accepted at the Workshop on Agent Behavior at COLM 2026. Each task drops an agent into a self-contained company environment — a file workspace plus simulated email, chat, calendar, issue-tracking, and commerce services exposed over the Model Context Protocol — and asks it to carry out routine work while an expert-written standard operating procedure, ranging from 20 to 124 pages, governs what it is and isn't allowed to do with no one checking each step. The strongest configuration tested, built on Claude Fable 5, reached a 36.2% pass rate under strict grading — 12.7 points ahead of the best configuration from any other provider, but still failing nearly two out of three tasks. The authors plan to publicly release the 65 tasks, the simulated environments, and the full evaluation harness.

Policy & compliance
EU AI Actdeadline 2 Aug

The EU AI Act's first real transparency deadline lands in three days

Why it matters: Article 50 hits ordinary, already-shipped generative AI features — chatbots, sentiment analysis, AI-generated marketing imagery — not just high-risk systems, so this is a compliance deadline most consumer-facing enterprises need to check against right now, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover for getting it wrong.

Detail & sources

Starting August 2, 2026, organizations become subject to the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations, which require providers and deployers to clearly disclose when a person is interacting with an AI system rather than a human — covering customer-service chatbots, automated sentiment analysis, and similar front-line generative features used across mid-market and enterprise businesses. AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video, or text content get a separate four-month extension, pushing their transparency-labeling deadline to December 2, 2026. Non-compliance exposes businesses to fines of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

CISA × Aristadeadline 30 Jul

CISA's deadline to patch an actively exploited, maximum-severity SD-WAN flaw is today

Why it matters: a CVSS 10.0, no-credentials-needed command injection in software Arista says is "exposed by default" is about as bad as network vulnerabilities get, and it's already under active exploitation — any enterprise running VeloCloud Orchestrator on-prem, including behind AI and data-center network builds, needs to confirm it patched by today's federal deadline, not just note the advisory.

Detail & sources

CVE-2026-16812, an OS command injection vulnerability in on-premises deployments of Arista's VeloCloud Orchestrator, carries a maximum CVSS score of 10.0: an attacker with only network access to the web interface, no credentials required, can execute arbitrary commands on the orchestrator host and reconfigure or disrupt every branch and data-center connection it manages. Arista confirmed active exploitation and said the product is exposed by default, meaning every unpatched instance is reachable by design. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, requiring federal civilian agencies to patch by July 30, 2026. Separately, Broadcom's July 29 VMware advisory (VMSA-2026-0006) patched two other critical flaws — a vCenter authentication bypass and a Syslog directory-traversal bug enabling remote code execution — both rated CVSS 9.8, with no workaround available.
