NvidiaReported 6–10 Jul

Nvidia's Kyber rack for Rubin Ultra slips past a year to 2028 on PCB problems — even as the stock closes above $5 trillion

Why it matters: a manufacturability snag in the midplane circuit board pushes Nvidia's flagship next-gen rack out a full generation cycle, creating a real opening for AMD and Google's in-house TPU silicon to win hyperscaler business — a rare crack in Nvidia's roadmap even as its market value keeps climbing.

Detail & sources

Research firm SemiAnalysis, as reported by CNBC on 6 July, said Nvidia's Kyber NVL144 rack — designed to house 144 Rubin Ultra chips per cabinet — has slipped more than 12 months, from 2027 to 2028, due to difficulty manufacturing a key PCB midplane. Nvidia told Tom's Hardware only that its "roadmap is intact." The delay lands the same week Nvidia's stock closed at a record, pushing its market capitalization above $5 trillion; the company's existing Rubin and Oberon racks remain in production and begin shipping to AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud this fall.

Policy & governance
IllinoisSigned 6 Jul

Illinois enacts the nation's strictest AI safety law, with annual audits and 72-hour incident reporting

Why it matters: it's the first state law to mandate independent third-party safety audits of frontier AI systems and a 72-hour (or 24-hour for imminent-danger) incident-reporting clock, backed by $1–3M civil penalties — a template other states are likely to copy as federal AI legislation stalls.

Detail & sources

Governor JB Pritzker signed SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, on 6 July. It requires developers of the largest advanced AI systems to publish frameworks describing how they assess "catastrophic risk" (incidents that could kill or seriously injure 50+ people or cause $1M+ in property damage), undergo annual independent third-party safety audits, and report critical safety incidents to the state within 72 hours (24 hours if imminent danger to life). It also creates confidential reporting channels and whistleblower protections. First violations carry $1M penalties, rising to $3M for subsequent violations. The law takes effect 1 January 2028.

Supply Chain / Git InfraProbing detected 7–12 Jul

Critical Gitea Docker auth-bypass flaw (CVSS 9.8) is now being actively probed by attackers

Why it matters: roughly 6,200 internet-facing Gitea instances trust a spoofable header by default, letting an unauthenticated attacker impersonate any known or guessable username and reach source code and secrets — patch to 1.26.3 today if you run Gitea's Docker image behind a reverse proxy.

Detail & sources

CVE-2026-20896 stems from Gitea's Docker image hard-coding REVERSE_PROXY_TRUSTED_PROXIES = *, so any client that reaches the container's HTTP port directly — not just the intended proxy — can spoof the X-WEBAUTH-USER header and impersonate any user. It was fixed in Gitea 1.26.3 (released last month). Cloud security firm Sysdig detected the first in-the-wild exploitation attempt from a ProtonVPN exit node roughly 13 days after disclosure; researchers describe the activity so far as reconnaissance rather than confirmed compromise, but broad automated scanning is expected to follow.

npm / Node.jsDisclosed 12 Jul

Two new node-tar CVEs let a crafted archive hang or disk-bomb any process that extracts it

Why it matters: node-tar sits deep in the npm dependency graph (npm CLI, build tooling, CI pipelines), so a single malicious or malformed archive can freeze a build server or exhaust its disk — check your lockfiles for tar below 7.5.19 today.

Detail & sources

CVE-2026-59873 (decompression/parse DoS): prior to 7.5.19, node-tar enforces no upper bound on total decompressed size, entry count, or decompression ratio during extraction, letting a small crafted gzip bomb exhaust disk space and CPU. CVE-2026-59874 (infinite loop): prior to 7.5.18, tar.replace accepts a checksum-valid header with a negative base-256-encoded entry size, causing the archive scanner to spin on the same header indefinitely. Both are fixed in the latest 7.5.x releases; neither affects npm's own package installs, which strip Link/SymlinkLink entries, but both affect any application or CI job that extracts untrusted tarballs directly.

Device-code phishing keeps compounding: 18 kits now in the wild, a 37.5x rise in detections since March

Why it matters: device-code phishing bypasses passwords, MFA, and even passkeys by attacking the OAuth authorization layer directly, and it has gone from a nation-state technique to commodity phishing-as-a-service in under two years — if your org allows the device-code login flow, restrict or monitor it now rather than relying on MFA alone.

Detail & sources

Push Security's latest tracking, published 11 July, counts at least 18 distinct device-code phishing kits and a 37.5x increase in phishing-page detections since March (up from a 15x rise reported in March), with every major adversary-in-the-middle phishing vendor now adding the technique to its platform. Kits impersonate DocuSign, SharePoint, OneDrive, calendar invites, and voicemail/eFax notifications, and bundle AI-generated lure copy and anti-analysis protections. Earlier campaigns built on this technique, such as the EvilTokens kit, have already hit 340+ Microsoft 365 organizations across five countries.
