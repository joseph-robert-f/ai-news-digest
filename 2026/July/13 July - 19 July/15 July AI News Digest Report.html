China × AI RegulationEffective 15 Jul

China's anthropomorphic-AI rules take effect, and ByteDance and Alibaba are already switching off companion features

Why it matters: this is Beijing's first rulebook aimed specifically at AI that simulates a relationship rather than performs a task, and it's forcing China's two biggest consumer AI products to gut a feature category overnight — a regulatory divergence from the US and EU that product teams building companion or persona features anywhere in the China market need to account for now.

Detail & sources

The Cyberspace Administration of China's Interim Measures for the Administration of Anthropomorphic Artificial Intelligence Interaction Services, jointly issued 10 April with the NDRC, MIIT, Ministry of Public Security, and SAMR, took effect 15 July. The rules require AI companion and persona services to continuously and prominently disclose that a user is interacting with AI, obtain parental consent for users under 14 and bar virtual intimate-relationship features for minors entirely, add anti-addiction interventions after two-hour sessions, and provide tiered escalation protocols for users showing signs of self-harm or acute distress. ByteDance's Doubao — China's most-used AI chatbot — shut down its custom-persona feature on 15 July, keeping existing configurations and chat histories in read-only mode until 15 October before permanent deletion. Alibaba's Qwen disabled its humanlike interactive-agent and user-created-agent functions on 10 July, with broader agent functions following on 15 July. The rules target companion-style personas specifically; task-oriented coding and productivity agents are unaffected.

Nvidia × Export ControlsReported 14 Jul

Nvidia halves its whitelist of approved Asian AI-chip buyers as Washington's diversion crackdown bites

Why it matters: Nvidia is now doing its own field due diligence — data-center visits, contract checks, end-user interviews — rather than relying on paperwork, which is a meaningful escalation in enforcement effort and a signal that regulators believe prior compliance checks weren't catching real diversion of Blackwell-class chips to China through third countries.

Detail & sources

The Financial Times reported, corroborated by multiple outlets on 14 July, that Nvidia has cut more than half of its previously approved Asian AI-chip customers from a new, stricter whitelist, hitting neo-cloud providers especially hard. The company has stepped up in-person diligence in Singapore, Malaysia, and Japan over recent months, sending staff to inspect data centers, verify contracts, and interview end users before granting or renewing approval. The move follows US Commerce Department guidance issued in May aimed at curbing advanced AI chips reaching overseas subsidiaries of Chinese companies, after concerns that Blackwell-class processors had reached Chinese-linked entities in countries such as Malaysia despite existing restrictions. Removed customers can reapply if they address the concerns that got them cut.

OpenAI × Apple14 Jul

OpenAI says it's "not aware of any evidence" Apple's trade-secret lawsuit has merit

Why it matters: this is OpenAI's first public response to Apple's 10 July suit, and it's a flat denial rather than a substantive rebuttal of the specific allegations (that hardware chief Tang Tan and other ex-Apple staff carried confidential silicon and on-device AI designs into OpenAI's hardware push) — expect the real contest to play out in discovery, not press statements.

Detail & sources

OpenAI said on 14 July, four days after Apple filed suit in the Northern District of California, that "while we take these allegations seriously, we're not aware of any evidence that this complaint has merit," adding that it "believe[s] in fair competition and allowing people the freedom to work wherever they choose." The statement does not address Apple's specific claims point by point, including a laptop-theft allegation referenced in the complaint. Apple is seeking damages and an injunction; the case remains in its early stages.

OpenAI × PolicyPublished 8 Jul

OpenAI publishes National Security Principles, drawing three explicit hard limits on government use of its models

Why it matters: as OpenAI expands government and defense work in cyber and biosecurity, it's getting ahead of the "will AI companies help build surveillance or autonomous-weapons capability" question with public, self-imposed limits rather than waiting for legislation to force the issue — a document that will now be the reference point every time a government-facing OpenAI deal draws scrutiny.

Detail & sources

OpenAI published its National Security Principles on 8 July, developed with input from outside national-security expert David Kris and internal listening sessions spanning research, safety, policy, and government-partnerships teams. The framework sets three hard limits: no use for mass domestic surveillance, no directing of autonomous weapons, and no high-stakes automated decisions made without meaningful human judgment. OpenAI frames the principles as enabling democratic governments to use its models defensively — protecting infrastructure, aiding cyber defense and biosecurity — while committing to deployments that reinforce democratic accountability rather than concentrate power. The publication lands as OpenAI expands work with the US government and allied partners on defensive national-security use cases.

Security & software engineering
Supply Chain / GitHub Actions14 Jul

Attackers hijack AsyncAPI's own GitHub Actions pipeline via a "pwn request" to backdoor npm packages with 2.9M weekly downloads

Why it matters: the attacker didn't need to steal a maintainer's credentials at all — a single malicious pull request among 37 opened as noise exploited a misconfigured pull_request_target workflow to steal the project's own privileged automation token, publish through the project's legitimate CI, and ship a multi-stage RAT that activates on import rather than install — a technique that defeats install-script mitigations like npm 12's new defaults entirely.

Detail & sources

On 14 July, an attacker opened 37 pull requests against the asyncapi/generator repository; one exploited a "pwn request" vulnerability class in a workflow that used pull_request_target (which runs with base-repository secrets) while checking out attacker-controlled pull-request code, letting the attacker exfiltrate a highly privileged personal access token. Using that token, the attacker pushed a malicious commit directly to the next branch at 06:58 UTC, triggering the legitimate release workflow to publish compromised versions of four packages under the @asyncapi namespace — including @asyncapi/specs, which alone accounts for roughly 2.7M of the packages' combined 2.9M weekly downloads. The payload is a hybrid info-stealer, crypto-wallet drainer, and remote access trojan that harvests browser-saved passwords and cookies, SSH keys, npm and GitHub tokens, AWS credentials, and macOS Keychain data, and maintains a live command-and-control channel; because it executes at require()/import time rather than at install, it isn't blocked by npm 12's new install-script defaults (covered in this digest on 14 July).

Vulnerability ManagementAdded to KEV 14 Jul

CISA adds two actively exploited SonicWall SMA1000 zero-days (CVSS 10.0) plus a Microsoft ADFS flaw to its KEV catalog

Why it matters: SonicWall itself confirmed multiple real incidents from these zero-days before a patch existed, and the unauthenticated SSRF bug alone is enough to compromise an internet-facing SMA1000 appliance — federal agencies have until 17 July to patch or disconnect, and any organization running SMA1000 for remote access should treat that deadline as the real one.

Detail & sources

CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 14 July, most notably CVE-2026-15409, a maximum-severity (CVSS 10.0) unauthenticated server-side request forgery flaw in the SMA1000 Appliance Work Place interface, and CVE-2026-15410, a high-severity (CVSS 7.2) post-authentication code-injection flaw in the SMA1000 Management Console that lets an authenticated admin run arbitrary OS commands. SonicWall disclosed both as zero-days already exploited in the wild, having investigated multiple confirmed customer incidents; fixes are available in hotfix builds 12.4.3-03453 and 12.5.0-02835. The same KEV batch added CVE-2026-56155, an access-control flaw in Microsoft Active Directory Federation Services. Per Binding Operational Directive 26-04, federal civilian agencies must remediate or disconnect affected systems by 17 July.
