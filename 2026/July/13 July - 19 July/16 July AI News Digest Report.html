Nvidia16 Jul

Nvidia plants a full-stack "physical AI" flag in Japan

Why it matters: Nvidia is converting its China lockout into a sovereign-AI land grab — wiring Japan's industrial giants onto its robotics stack now sets the default platform for the next decade of factory automation.

Detail & sources

On a two-day Tokyo visit, Jensen Huang unveiled Cosmos 3 Edge, a new model for robots and vision agents to perceive and navigate the physical world in real time, alongside a coalition of Japanese manufacturers. Nvidia and Noetra — Japan's "national physical-AI team," which includes Sony and Honda — announced a national AI super-factory powered by 27,500 Rubin GPUs. Fanuc and Yaskawa Electric signed on for AI-powered robotics, and Fujitsu, Hitachi and Kawasaki Heavy Industries joined the coalition. Huang pressed "sovereign AI" in a pitch to the Takaichi administration.

Google16 Jul

Gemini 3.5 Pro misses a third deadline; Google weighs a stopgap

Why it matters: Google's flagship keeps slipping while GPT‑5.6 and Grok 4.5 are already shipping — every delay cedes the enterprise-reasoning tier to rivals, and the market is pricing it in.

Detail & sources

After scrapping the base model and rebuilding from scratch — engineers reportedly hit structural failures in recursive tool-calling and SVG generation — Google's ~17 July target slipped again on hallucination and reliability gaps, and reporting now says Google is eyeing a stopgap release. Alphabet shed roughly $225B in market value on the news. The competitive backdrop is harsher than June's slip: GPT‑5.6 Sol and Grok 4.5 both went public on 9 July.

OpenAI's first device: a screen-free speaker built to "feel alive"

Why it matters: OpenAI is betting the post-phone AI interface is an ambient, humanlike companion — not an app — putting it on a collision course with Apple both in the living room and in court.

Detail & sources

Bloomberg and Fortune detailed the first product from io, the design firm OpenAI bought from Jony Ive for $6.5B: a portable, screenless smart speaker with mechanical elements that move to create a sense that it is alive, running a more advanced version of OpenAI's new GPT‑Live voice mode. Ex-Apple design lead Evans Hankey leads development; a commercial launch is targeted for 2027. It lands days after Apple sued OpenAI on 10 July for trade-secret theft.

Policy16 Jul

Anthropic backs tougher state AI laws as OpenAI pushes federal preemption

Why it matters: the two leading labs are now openly lobbying opposite directions — the outcome decides whether US AI compliance becomes one federal standard or a rising 50-state patchwork.

Detail & sources

Anthropic endorsed California's SB 53, New York's RAISE Act, an Illinois bill requiring independent third-party audits, and a Massachusetts measure, arguing Congress should not preempt state law unless it passes a federal standard at least as strong. OpenAI's global-affairs chief is pushing the opposite — a single, lighter national standard with immediate preemption, a "reverse federalism" play to get states to mirror one another.

Markets16 Jul

TSMC lifts capex to the top of its range on "extremely robust" AI demand

Why it matters: the industry's clearest demand signal just got stronger — TSMC sees agentic AI, not just chatbots, driving a supply-constrained compute build-out through 2026.

Detail & sources

In Q2 results, TSMC raised its 2026 capex outlook to the high end of its $52–56B range and reaffirmed full-year revenue growth of more than 30% in USD, citing "extremely robust" AI-chip demand and the shift from generative to agentic AI. Advanced nodes stay supply-constrained — more N3 capacity in Taiwan, Arizona and Japan, with N2 ramping as the next growth platform. Even so, the broader AI-stock rally stalled on the day despite the beat.

Coinbase15 Jul

Coinbase says 95–100% of its code is now AI-assisted

Why it matters: a regulated, security-sensitive public company reporting near-total AI-written code is a hard datapoint on how far agentic coding has moved from hype to default — with humans retreating to oversight.

Detail & sources

Platform head Rob Witoff said 95–100% of Coinbase's code is now written by or with LLMs, up from roughly 40% in February; engineers run 5–10 coding agents at once. Humans stay central for cryptography and core security, and Coinbase projects AI could handle work equivalent to 100,000 employees by 2030. It's a striking adoption curve for a public company in a heavily regulated sector.
