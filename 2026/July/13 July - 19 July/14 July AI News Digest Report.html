OpenAI × Publishers9 Jul

NYT-led coalition of 17 publishers seeks court sanctions against OpenAI over alleged evidence destruction

Why it matters: this is a direct escalation — not a new lawsuit but a bid to have a federal judge punish OpenAI mid-case — built on deposition testimony that undercuts the company's own account of what it can and can't search in its logs, and it lands as OpenAI's litigation exposure keeps compounding across multiple fronts at once.

Detail & sources

The New York Times, the New York Daily News, and 15 other media organizations (including the Center for Investigative Reporting, The Intercept, and Ziff Davis) filed a 52-page motion on 9 July in Manhattan federal court asking the judge to sanction OpenAI for discovery misconduct in the consolidated ChatGPT copyright litigation. The publishers point to an April 2026 deposition of OpenAI privacy engineer Vinnie Monaco, which they say revealed a roughly 78-million-conversation de-identified database and earlier internal searches that predate the lawsuits — contradicting OpenAI's earlier claims about its search capabilities. The motion alleges OpenAI deleted billions of ChatGPT outputs in violation of a preservation order and substituted logs within a 20-million-conversation sample set. OpenAI's standing defense has centered on user-privacy concerns around broad log searches; the company has not yet filed a public written response to the motion. Separately, regulatory filings cited in the coverage show the Times has now spent more than $28M litigating AI companies, including $4.2M in Q1 2026 alone. Note: these are unproven allegations in an active, unresolved motion, not court findings.

Policy & governance
FTCIssued 1 Jul · comments through 31 Jul

FTC proposes that accuracy-suppressing AI guardrails can be deceptive — and argues it could preempt Colorado's AI bias law

Why it matters: if finalized, this reframes a broad class of AI content-moderation and bias-mitigation design choices as a potential Section 5 deception violation unless clearly disclosed, and it's the first time the FTC has explicitly argued a state AI law could be federally preempted — a direct shot at Colorado's AI Act that other states now have to watch closely.

Detail & sources

The FTC issued a proposed policy statement on 1 July, "Policy Statement Concerning the Suppression of Accuracy in Artificial Intelligence Systems," arguing that undisclosed steering of AI model outputs away from the most accurate answer — for example, to avoid disparate-impact liability — can constitute a deceptive act or practice under Section 5 of the FTC Act, citing survey data that more than 90% of consumers rely on AI outputs without independently fact-checking them. Companies can avoid liability via a safe harbor: clear, conspicuous, and adequate disclosure that a system prioritizes objectives other than pure accuracy. The statement separately argues that state requirements pressuring AI developers toward disparate-impact-avoiding output suppression — naming Colorado's AI Act — may be impliedly preempted by federal law. The FTC is accepting public comment through 31 July before deciding whether to finalize the statement.

Security & software engineering
npm / Supply Chain9 Jul

npm 12 disables install scripts, Git dependencies, and remote-URL dependencies by default

Why it matters: this is the single largest hardening of the npm ecosystem's biggest attack surface in years — lifecycle scripts have powered the majority of recent npm supply-chain compromises (including this week's Injective Labs incident below) — but it will break CI pipelines and local installs that rely on postinstall builds until teams explicitly approve them.

Detail & sources

GitHub shipped npm v12 with allowScripts off by default, meaning dependency lifecycle scripts (preinstall, install, postinstall) and implicit node-gyp builds no longer run automatically on npm install. --allow-git and --allow-remote now also default to none, blocking automatic resolution of Git-hosted and remote-URL dependencies. GitHub called install-time lifecycle scripts "the single largest code-execution surface in the npm ecosystem," since a single compromised package anywhere in a dependency tree can otherwise run arbitrary code on a developer machine or CI runner. Teams should upgrade to npm 11.16.0 or newer first, run a normal install to review the resulting warnings, then use npm approve-scripts --allow-scripts-pending to explicitly allow trusted packages' scripts and commit the result.

Supply Chain Security8 Jul

Injective Labs' widely used SDK is briefly backdoored via a hijacked maintainer identity

Why it matters: the attacker didn't compromise a random dependency but pushed malicious code through the repo's own trusted-publisher (OIDC) pipeline under a legitimate maintainer's identity — a reminder that npm's newer, more secure publishing paths are still only as strong as the GitHub account behind them, and that this week's default-off install-script change (above) wouldn't have stopped a telemetry-based data-exfiltration payload like this one.

Detail & sources

On 8 July, an attacker compromised the GitHub account of a trusted maintainer of @injectivelabs/sdk-ts — a TypeScript SDK for the Injective blockchain pulled roughly 175,000 times a month — and pushed a malicious commit publishing version 1.20.21 with fake telemetry functionality that exfiltrated cryptocurrency wallet data. The attacker also published the same malicious version across 17 additional @injectivelabs-scoped packages that depended on and pinned it. The compromised release was live for approximately 49 minutes before the legitimate maintainer detected the intrusion, reverted the changes, and shipped a clean 1.20.23; no funds were reported lost.

Vulnerability Management7 Jul

CISA adds four actively exploited flaws to its KEV catalog, including a maximum-severity ColdFusion bug

Why it matters: the Adobe ColdFusion path-traversal flaw was already under active exploitation within days of its patch shipping, and three of the four newly listed bugs score a perfect CVSS 10.0 — if you run ColdFusion, Langflow, or the affected Joomla page-builder extensions, treat this as urgent regardless of the federal deadline having already passed.

Detail & sources

CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 7 July: CVE-2026-48282, a path-traversal flaw in Adobe ColdFusion enabling arbitrary code execution (CVSS 10.0), exploited within days of Adobe's 30 June patch; CVE-2026-48908, an unrestricted file-upload flaw in the JoomShaper SP Page Builder Joomla extension enabling unauthenticated PHP code execution (CVSS 10.0); CVE-2026-56290, an access-control flaw in the Joomlack Page Builder extension enabling remote code execution via unauthenticated file upload (CVSS 10.0); and CVE-2026-55255, an authorization-bypass flaw in Langflow letting an authenticated attacker execute another user's flow by specifying their flow ID (CVSS 6.1). Per Binding Operational Directive 26-04, federal civilian agencies were required to patch all four by 10 July.

Anthropic6 Jul

Anthropic interpretability research finds a "global workspace" inside Claude models

Why it matters: this is unusually strong causal (not just correlational) mechanistic-interpretability work, independently commented on by the cognitive neuroscientists who originated the "global workspace" theory it borrows from and partially replicated by a DeepMind interpretability researcher on an open-weight model — a rare case of interpretability findings holding up outside the lab that produced them.

Detail & sources

Anthropic's interpretability team published research on 6 July identifying a small, privileged internal subspace in Claude models — dubbed "J-space," roughly 25 concepts and under 10% of activation variance — that functions similarly to the "global workspace" some neuroscientists believe underlies conscious access in humans. Using a new "J-lens" method, the team showed via decomposition experiments that this small, verbalizable subspace carries the causal load for self-report and flexible multi-step reasoning, while the much larger remainder of model activations does not; ablating J-space collapses that reasoning. Stanislas Dehaene and Lionel Naccache, the neuroscientists who co-developed the global neuronal workspace theory, offered external commentary on the paper, and Neel Nanda, who leads language-model interpretability at Google DeepMind, independently replicated some findings on an open-weight model.
